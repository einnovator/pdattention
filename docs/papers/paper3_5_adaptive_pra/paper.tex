\begin{abstract}
Progressive Retrieval Attention (PRA) separates conceptual memory discovery from physical
native-key/value (K/V) activation.  This paper asks how much PRA effort a query should receive and
when a bounded second attempt is justified.  We first evaluate three coherent effort profiles, then
open their coupling into independently selectable interpretation, search, and admission controls.

The factorized study replays frozen native graph and query scores for 95 validation and 65 held-out
HotpotQA/QASPER example--seed units.  It evaluates 792 valid configurations per unit over query
facets $F$, roots $R$, successor breadth $K$, hop depth $H$, conceptual budget $B_s$, and physical
K/V admission budget $B_{kv}$.  On held-out HotpotQA, the factorized oracle preserves complete-chain
quality 1.000 while reducing mean abstract cost from 34.18 for the cheapest sufficient E0/E1/E2
profile to 19.98.  On the 24 QASPER units solved by both action spaces, cost falls from 16.11 to 8.95;
factorization additionally solves one unit that no profile solves, yielding .833 versus .800 overall.
Thus profile coupling hides substantial lower-cost combinations, although no learned router yet
predicts those combinations reliably.

Five-seed factorized controllers make that limitation explicit.  R0 profile routing obtains .631
chain quality at cost 24.29.  Independent, conservative, signed-hash query, and autoregressive heads
reduce cost to 16.13--18.06 but quality to .486--.560 because under-allocation rises.  Parameter
accuracy is therefore not the endpoint.  A one-retry evaluator-side correction audit raises quality
from .631 to the factorized ceiling .923, correcting all 15 initial HotpotQA failures and four of nine
QASPER failures at 2.49 mean added cost; eight HotpotQA and QASPER cases use a single root/facet
change, while compound fallback remains necessary elsewhere.  This is retry headroom, not a learned
online policy.

Normalized candidate accounting exposes a different controller tradeoff from the abstract cost
alone.  On QASPER, the factorized oracle recovers .917 of evidence parents while admitting 10.9\%
of candidates; targeted retry reaches the same recall at 19.0\%.  On HotpotQA, targeted retry raises
evidence-parent recall from .733 to .948, but expands admission from 69.7\% to 75.6\%.  These are
identity-level discovery and native-K/V activation measurements, not generated-answer gains.

A separate four-dataset study treats retrieval representation itself as an action.  It replays 132
frozen Paper-2.6 examples, of which 120 have a labelled successor decision.  Validation selects
exact root search, reaching .066 held-out root recall versus .104 for the per-example root oracle.
For the 68 held-out examples with successor labels, a fixed BM25-root/native-semantic-successor pair
reaches .030 recall; independently predicted root and successor methods reach .032, an interpretable
cascade reaches .048, and the joint method oracle reaches .143.  The method space therefore has
real headroom, but the present small controller does not capture it.  Learned targeted retry raises
.032 only to .037, while an oracle correction reaches .143.  These are matched-budget discovery
metrics, not generated-answer or K/V-materialization gains.

We then strengthen request/reply with global, structural, multiscale, graph, and hierarchical
structural-to-graph facets and execute the complete matched surface: 29,700 actions over the original
58 validation and 74 test identities, with five root and five successor methods.  A validation-fixed
global policy reaches .205 test evidence recall.  Five-seed query-only one-shot control reaches
$.180\pm.022$ without graph and $.180\pm.023$ with graph.  A learned single callback after root
selection falls to .168; a validation-tuned uncertainty rule reaches $.188\pm.024$ while invoking
graph refinement on 16.5\% of cases.  Complete-action and stage-wise evaluator oracles both reach
.365 exactly.  Their equality is important: on this measured Cartesian surface, observing root
state creates no additional oracle action, and learned callbacks do not justify serving complexity.
A stratified eight-example frozen Qwen decode check maps four conceptual selections to 128 native
K/V tokens across eight consumer layers.  One-shot and threshold policies raise exact match/token
F1 from .375 without memory to .500; the callback result is .375.  This small confirmation verifies
materialization and generation but is not a benchmark-quality answer comparison.

Query interpretation is itself an action.  Explicit and structural regions remove recent-head
serialization sensitivity in matched controls and remain stable through 8K following tokens.
Structural selection is perfect on six semi-natural fixtures but falls to .400 under five deliberate
role decoys, while the competing recent-head interpretation remains correct there.  The result
motivates disagreement-aware reinterpretation rather than unconditional structural replacement.
We also ask whether the frozen Qwen3-0.6B backbone should read its own question before allocating
PRA effort.  Four-fold grouped validation selects the last question-token embedding, but its
five-seed held-out quality is .668 at total cost 27.20, below the observable profile router's .702
at 28.25.
The validation-selected source-plus-query upper bound reaches only .674 while paying a 256-token
prefill.  Thus model-native query state does not yet justify replacing observable R0 or introducing
an external encoder.  Exact prefix continuation does, however, show that representations captured
before the first PRA consumer can be reused without recomputing the query.
All primary quality results are frozen-score evidence-chain endpoints, not generated-answer scores.
Kernel, cache, batching, and serving-engine optimization are handed to Papers 5.5 and 6.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A fixed memory policy is wrong in two directions.  A narrow policy wastes quality on questions
whose evidence is dispersed or connected by several relations.  A broad policy wastes search,
transfer, K/V capacity, and attention competition on questions that can be answered from one
compact region.  The relevant systems question is therefore not only which memory should be
retrieved, but how much effort the current query justifies.

PRA provides several independent control surfaces.  A runtime may form one or several query
facets, retain one or several roots, expand different numbers of neighbors and hops, search at
different layers, and materialize anything from a compact evidence interval to a broader source
region.  Paper~3 showed that conceptual breadth and physical disclosure are not the same budget:
once identity is fixed, exact native-K/V evidence can outperform a whole parent while using far
fewer physical states.  The present work turns those controls into an adaptive inference contract.

The central test is
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\mathrm{adaptive}} \approx Q_{\mathrm{always\mbox{-}high}}
 \quad\text{while}\quad
 \mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{adaptive}}] < C_{\mathrm{always\mbox{-}high}},
 \label{eq:adaptive-goal}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ is held-out output or retrieval quality and $C$ includes conceptual search, physical K/V,
generation retries, and measured latency.  Equation~\ref{eq:adaptive-goal} is not guaranteed on
every dataset.  If almost every query is hard, the correct controller should spend the high budget.

This paper makes thirteen contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item It defines a public PRA control vector and freezes three monotonic effort profiles rather
than predicting unconstrained continuous parameters.
\item It implements hand-rule and learned minimum-effort controllers using only observable query,
routing, output, and memory features; evaluator labels are rejected by the serving API.
\item It provides manual and automatic retry modes with incremental state reuse, hard budgets,
semantic answer consistency, and structured decision traces.
\item It evaluates validation-frozen adaptive compute, calibration, retry recovery, and oracle
minimum effort on held-out HotpotQA and QASPER traces.
\item It makes query-region location and extent explicit, supports structured prompt roles, and
tests matched query-first, query-last, query-middle, log, and URL-list layouts.
\item It measures profile quantization regret over 792 valid factorized configurations per unit,
including token-weighted evidence precision and recall and separate search/admission budgets.
\item It retrains profile, independent-head, signed-hash query, conservative, and autoregressive
routers on validation-derived factorized targets rather than inherited profile labels.
\item It evaluates bounded single-control correction and compound fallback as explicit retry
upper bounds, recording wrong-to-correct, correct-to-broken, extra work, and action identity.
\item It compares signed hashing, token embeddings, early/middle/final query-only states, native
Q/K geometry, and an expensive contextual upper bound under grouped validation selection.
\item It tests coherent group profiles against global, independent, and autoregressive factorized
targets, and supplies an exact pre-consumer query-prefill reuse path with cost accounting.
\item It makes root and successor retrieval representations independent controller actions, imports
the Paper-2.6 action contract by content hash, and compares fixed, cascade, learned, and oracle
method policies on four natural evidence tasks.
\item It integrates tensor-native semantic query graphs into the same one-shot request/reply action,
adds hierarchical syntactic-to-graph facets and per-facet channel policies, and reports graph cost,
overlap, five-seed controller, and oracle-headroom measurements.
\item It implements one opt-in \texttt{ROOT\_SELECTED} callback, executes a matched 29,700-action
root/successor surface, compares learned and threshold controls under standard and
leave-one-dataset-out splits, and confirms selected identities through native-K/V materialization
and frozen decode.
\end{enumerate}

Full-weight PRA retraining is deliberately absent.  That intervention belongs to Paper~4.  Here the
scientific object is adaptive inference over frozen routing and consumer behavior.

\section{Adaptive PRA as a Control Problem}

\subsection{Control vector}

At attempt $t$, the runtime selects
\begin{equation}
 a_t=(\mathcal Q,F_m,P_c,\theta_g,S_r,S_s,F,R,K,H,B_s,B_{kv},\theta,L_s,L_c,G,M),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal Q$ is one or more functional query regions, $S_r$ and $S_s$ are the root and
successor search representations, $F$ is the facet policy, $R$ retained roots, $K$ candidate
neighbors per expansion, $H$ hop depth, $B_s$ conceptual search budget, $B_{kv}$ physical native-K/V budget, $\theta$ a closure
threshold, $L_s$ search layers, $L_c$ consumer layers, $G$ routing/materialization granularity, and
$M$ disclosure policy.  We separate $B_s$ and $B_{kv}$ because Paper~3 found that broad search can
remain compatible with compact evidence-only activation.  We also do not force $S_r=S_s$: an
admitted chunk changes the successor query state and can expose an address best resolved in another
representation.
Here $F_m\in\{\text{global},\text{syntactic},\text{multiscale},\text{graph},
\text{syntactic}\rightarrow\text{graph}\}$ is the facet-construction mode, $P_c$ is the coarse
partition policy, and $\theta_g$ denotes the query-graph similarity, threshold, top-$k$, and
component constraints.  Keeping $F_m$ separate from facet count prevents a controller from treating
``four windows'' and ``four graph communities'' as the same interpretation action.

The first public implementation uses three profiles (Table~\ref{tab:profiles}).  They are not
universal hyperparameters; they are a stable SDK contract and an initial experimental ladder.

Escalation is monotonic in permitted facets, roots, neighbors, hops, conceptual budget, K/V budget,
and layer sets.  A lower $\theta$ is broader.  Code rejects a ladder in which a later profile removes
a prior resource.  Monotonic permission does not imply monotonic answer quality: extra memory may
dilute attention, so retries must also record correct-to-incorrect transitions.

\subsection{Observable state and leakage boundary}

The controller may observe cheap query statistics, score gaps, routing entropy, root competition,
facet agreement, frontier shape, newly discovered memory, path convergence, output entropy,
answer log probability and margin, retry consistency, active K/V, memory-attention ratio, and a
relevance-density proxy.  Ground-truth answers, oracle identities, gold evidence, and evidence
recall are evaluator fields.  They cannot enter the serving feature constructor.

For vocabulary distribution $p_t$, token entropy is
\begin{equation}
 H_t=-\sum_v p_t(v)\log p_t(v),
 \qquad \bar H = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}H_t.
\end{equation}
Entropy is one signal.  A fluent wrong answer can have low entropy.  We therefore compare
entropy-only, answer-margin, routing-only, and combined predictors rather than defining confidence
as a single scalar by fiat.

\subsection{Minimum-effort prediction}

For validation example $i$, let
\begin{equation}
 e_i^*=\min\{e\in\{E0,E1,E2\}: Q_i(e)\ge q_i^*\},
\end{equation}
where $q_i^*$ is the target quality criterion.  If no profile succeeds, $E2$ is retained as the
bounded fallback.  The oracle $e_i^*$ is used only to train or analyze the controller.  At test time,
a standardized ridge classifier predicts one profile from the initial observable feature vector.
The classifier is deliberately small, deterministic, and serializable without a new runtime
dependency.

Controller regret is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{regret}_i=C(\hat e_i)-C(e_i^*)
\end{equation}
subject to reporting whether $Q_i(\hat e_i)$ matches $Q_i(e_i^*)$.  Negative regret without matched
quality is not a saving.

\subsection{Query-region discovery: separating intent from position}

The runtime first asks which prompt region specifies the retrieval objective and only then asks how
to facet that region.  These are distinct decisions:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{prompt}\rightarrow\mathcal Q=\{[s_j,e_j)\}_j
 \rightarrow(\{q_1,\ldots,q_F\},S_r)\rightarrow\mathrm{roots}
 \rightarrow(S_s,\mathrm{search})\rightarrow\mathrm{admit}\rightarrow\mathrm{evaluate}.
\end{equation}
Functional roles include instruction, query, current state, context, references, history, and tool
or log output.  Position does not determine role.  Explicit token spans or caller-labeled segments
are authoritative.  Raw text falls back to a deterministic structural ladder that recognizes
question/instruction headings and punctuation while discounting code fences, logs, context labels,
and URL lists.  A retry may reinterpret a recent-head cue as a structural or multi-region cue before
increasing $R$, $K$, $H$, or K/V budget.

The selection records start, end, region count, method, confidence, and score margin.  Multiple
regions support a persistent instruction plus a narrower current question; session mode selects the
smallest current query state rather than the whole conversation.  The heuristic is deliberately a
baseline.  A learned span scorer is justified only if explicit-span headroom remains after structural
selection on natural prompts.

\subsection{Learned effort routing: profiles to interacting heads}

All controller families target minimum sufficient effort rather than the highest-scoring action:
\begin{equation}
 a_i^*=\arg\min_a C(a)\quad\text{subject to}\quad Q_i(a)\ge q_i^*.
\end{equation}
R0 selects one validation-frozen profile.  R1 uses a shared feature MLP and independent categorical
heads.  R2 adds a cached query-region representation.  R3A predicts controls autoregressively in the
order $\mathcal Q\rightarrow S_r\rightarrow F\rightarrow R\rightarrow S_s\rightarrow K
\rightarrow H\rightarrow B_s\rightarrow B_{kv}$.  The method heads are added only as measured
categorical targets; interaction-aware expansion remains gated by Section~\ref{sec:adaptive-search-methods}.
Integer controls and thresholds use finite categorical values; no Cartesian action class
is constructed.  The first comparison inherits profile-derived labels; the decisive comparison is
retrained later on independently measured factorized-oracle targets.
For independent heads,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\sum_j\lambda_j\,\mathrm{CE}(\hat a_j,a_j^*).
\end{equation}
Evaluation reports each head, under- and over-allocation, quality regret, cost regret, calibration,
and latency.  Joint exact match is secondary to the held-out quality/expected-effort frontier.

\subsection{State machine}

Attempt zero uses the selected profile or factorized action.  A manual caller may name low, medium,
or high effort, provide individual controls, and request one bounded retry.  Automatic mode applies
a calibrated stop rule.  If it fails, the corrective action may reinterpret the query, widen facets,
change root or successor representation, increase roots, neighbors, hops, or search budget, or
change K/V admission while preserving compatible
roots, search results, K/V state, and prefix state.  Search budget, active K/V, retry count, and
latency are hard caps.

The full controller cycle is therefore
\[
\mathrm{QUERY/SESSION}\rightarrow\mathrm{INTERPRET}\rightarrow\mathrm{SEARCH}
\rightarrow\mathrm{ADMIT}\rightarrow\mathrm{EVALUATE}\rightarrow\mathrm{targeted\ retry}.
\]

The public transition is:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
profile = controller.choose(observable_features)
while True:
    result = execute(profile, reusable_state=previous)
    stop, signals = stop_policy(result.features, result.error_probability)
    trace.append(public_decision(result, profile, signals))
    if stop or budget_exhausted(profile, trace):
        return result, trace
    profile = targeted_correction(profile, observable_failure_signals)
    previous = result
\end{lstlisting}

Answers across attempts are compared with normalized token F1.  This is a cheap semantic proxy, not
a full entailment judge.  The runtime records initially-wrong-to-correct, initially-correct-to-wrong,
and no-change retries separately.

\subsection{Structured traces}

A trace contains attempt index, profile and control vector, active K/V, selected-parent count,
incorrect-answer probability, routing entropy, answer margin, consistency, search/materialization/
generation time, reuse counts, query-region bounds, confidence, method and expansion count,
escalation signals, and stop reason.  It does not contain hidden chain-of-thought.  Evaluator
outcomes live in experiment rows outside the controller feature path.

\subsection{Control/execution boundary}

Let $Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n_q\times d}$ be native query states and
$K_m\in\mathbb{R}^{n_m\times d}$ indexed memory keys.  Exact batched search computes
\begin{equation}
 S=QK_m^\top,\qquad I=\operatorname{TopK}(S,k).
\end{equation}
This equation defines the search work exposed to the controller.  How a later runtime compiles or
indexes it is outside the adaptive-policy contribution; approximation is acceptable only under a
separate matched-recall systems gate.

\subsection{Search/admission boundary}

Conceptual search returns source-parent identities.  Admission maps a bounded subset to native K/V
with shape $[B,H,T,D_h]$.  The adaptive trace must record both the searched parent budget and the
physically admitted token count.  PagedAttention controls where K/V is stored~\cite{kwon2023vllm};
PRA control decides which logical memory becomes active.  Physical layout optimization is deferred.

\section{Experimental Protocol}

\subsection{Adaptive-compute cohort}

The controller study reuses frozen Paper-2.5 semantic exploration traces.  The selected root policy
is seed agreement at .6; transition policy is fixed Top-4.  Fractions .1, .2, and .4 instantiate E0,
E1, and E2.  There are 95 validation and 65 held-out example--seed units across HotpotQA and QASPER.
Validation chooses the hand rule, learned classifier, failure predictor, and retry threshold.  No
threshold is changed after held-out evaluation.

The quality endpoint is complete evidence-chain recovery.  This is a routing endpoint, not generated
answer accuracy.  Output confidence is therefore evaluated separately with Paper~3's controlled
model, using its validation/held-out split and actual prediction entropy, NLL, answer margin, and
correctness.  Keeping the two cohorts separate avoids fabricating a joint output/routing signal that
was not measured.

Abstract effort is deterministic:
\begin{equation}
 C(e)=R K(H+1)+B_c+B_{kv}/64.
\end{equation}
A retry pays the final monotonic profile plus a fixed controlled regeneration charge of two units
per additional attempt.  Measured routing latency is reported separately.

\subsection{Factorized-control cohort}

The same 95/65 grouped split is replayed from frozen Qwen native adjacency, query-facet scores, source
spans, and evidence groups.  Validation supplies factorized router targets; held-out targets are
evaluator-only.  The full valid lattice is executed on both partitions so the held-out oracle is an
evaluation ceiling, not a pruning input.  Pareto dominance maximizes complete-chain quality, evidence
recall, and evidence precision while minimizing abstract cost, active K/V, and transition comparisons.

R0 is trained against the minimum sufficient profile.  R1, R2, and R3A are trained against the
minimum-cost factorized action.  R2's 32-dimensional signed-hash input uses the actual question text.
All neural controllers use five fixed initialization seeds.  The retry audit begins from one frozen
R0 seed and permits one single-control correction or a separately labeled compound fallback.

\subsection{Self-encoded query-router cohort}

The self-router add-on keeps the factorized surface, identities, partitions, and five controller
seeds fixed.  It freezes Qwen3-0.6B at revision
\texttt{c1899de289a04d12100db370d81485cdf75e47ca}.  For each of the 32 unique questions, the normal
question-only chat prompt is encoded with memory disabled; pooling is restricted to the explicit
question span.  The sweep includes token embeddings, hidden states after layers 4, 7, 14, 18, and
28, and native Q/K states after Qwen's per-head RMS normalization and before RoPE at representative
layers.  Mean pooling is primary; last
token is tested only for embeddings and layers 14 and 28.  A 256-token ordinary source-plus-question
prompt supplies a contextual diagnostic upper bound.

Every high-dimensional representation is compressed by a 16-dimensional PCA fitted only on unique
validation identities.  Four-fold validation cross-validation, grouped by example identity, selects
representations and compact codebooks; the 65 held-out example--seed units do not participate.
The same two-layer 32-unit profile head and five initialization seeds are used across sources.  The
target-granularity gate compares validation-derived global profiles, coherent interpret/search/admit
groups, independent categorical values, and the sequence $F\rightarrow R\rightarrow K\rightarrow
H\rightarrow B$.

A separate query prepass through depth $k$ is charged
\begin{equation}
 C_q=\frac{T_q\max(1,k)}{64L},\qquad C_{\mathrm{total}}=C(a)+C_q,
 \label{eq:self-prefill-cost}
\end{equation}
where $T_q$ includes the question chat wrapper and $L=28$.  We also report cumulative warmed CUDA
event latency on a GTX~950M.  If capture stops before the first possible PRA consumer (layer 24),
the exact intermediate state may be reused and the additional $C_q$ is zero.  Tests require bitwise
parity with uninterrupted Qwen execution and verify that the prepass mutates no parameter or cache.

\subsection{Inherited measurement scope}

Search, gather, page, cache, and batching benchmarks run on CPU with PyTorch 2.12.1.  Exact search
uses 32 queries, 64-dimensional keys, Top-8, and 256--4,096 memories.  Kernel tests use 8 heads and
head dimension 32.  Page tests store 1,537 tokens and select 128 clustered or dispersed tokens.
Batch tests use 1--32 heterogeneous requests.  We report medians and mark HBM estimates as
extrapolated.

These earlier CPU components and held-out Qwen observations are retained only as provenance for the
Paper-5.5 handoff.  They are not used to select factorized actions and are not adaptive end-to-end
serving measurements.

\subsection{Inherited baseline discipline}

The matched synthetic corpus contains 1,024 documents and 200 two-hop queries.  The query retrieves
a bridge; the answer evidence is linked from that bridge.  Native context, truncation, one-shot RAG,
multi-query RAG, iterative RAG, fixed PRA, and adaptive PRA share the corpus and retrieval geometry.
This controlled construction tests cost accounting and the need for iteration.  It does not replace
a full production RAG evaluation~\cite{lewis2020rag,trivedi2023ircot}.

H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o}, SnapKV~\cite{li2024snapkv}, StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2023streamingllm}, and
ClusterKV~\cite{liu2024clusterkv} are represented by mechanism-faithful controlled selectors at
matched active-K/V budgets.  No upstream kernel is claimed.  Sparse architectural methods are
discussed rather than experimentally mixed with incompatible checkpoints.

\subsection{Retry recovery is not free}

Cheap-first retry corrects 31.4\% of HotpotQA units that fail E0 and 10.0\% of QASPER units.  It does
not break an initially correct unit in this trace.  However, HotpotQA reaches E2 on every request and
pays two extra generation attempts, raising cost from 152.0 to 156.0.  QASPER stops early on two
units but still escalates 93.3\%, reducing cost only to 141.9.  Retry is valuable as a recovery and
fallback mechanism; the learned initial profile is the main source of QASPER savings.

This result also distinguishes routing reuse from generation reuse.  Roots and K/V states can be
preserved incrementally, but a changed answer may still require regeneration.  A serving claim must
measure discarded tokens and prefix-cache reuse rather than treating monotonic search as free.

\section{Factorized Control Results}

\subsection{Measured action lattice and cost}

The preliminary multi-head comparison cannot answer whether profiles are the right action unit:
every target field was copied from E0, E1, or E2.  We therefore replay the frozen native adjacency
and query-facet scores over
\begin{equation}
 (F,R,K,H,B_s,B_{kv})\in
 \{1,2,4\}\times\{1,2,4\}\times\{2,4,8\}\times
 \{0,1,2,3\}\times\{2,4,8\}\times\{2,4,8\}.
\end{equation}
Configurations with $B_s<R$ or $B_{kv}<R$ are invalid, leaving 792 measured actions per
example--seed unit.  Query-region policy is fixed to structural, native edge admission is open,
terminal-goal stopping is closed, and best-first traversal is fixed.  These controls isolate profile
coupling rather than reopening every Paper-2.5 routing choice.

For each execution, root scoring compares $F$ facets with all source parents; graph search records
actual native proposals; and physical admission preserves roots before admitting discovered parents
in search order.  The normalized accounting used only for within-study ordering is
\begin{equation}
 C(a)=F+N_{\mathrm{transition}}+B_s+\frac{T_{kv}}{64},
 \label{eq:factorized-cost}
\end{equation}
where $T_{kv}$ is the number of materialized native-K/V source tokens.  The artifacts also retain
unscaled root comparisons $F N_{\mathrm{parents}}$, transition comparisons, conceptual parents,
K/V tokens, and wall time.  Thus Equation~\ref{eq:factorized-cost} is auditable rather than a latency
surrogate.

Physical evidence metrics are token weighted:
\begin{equation}
 P_{E,kv}=\frac{\text{materialized evidence K/V tokens}}
                 {\text{all materialized K/V tokens}},\qquad
 R_{E,kv}=\frac{\text{materialized evidence K/V tokens}}
                 {\text{required evidence K/V tokens}}.
\end{equation}
Complete-chain quality requires every evidence group to be represented after physical admission.
No generated answer exists for these newly factorized configurations, so no output oracle is imputed.

\section{Retrieval Representation as an Adaptive Action}
\label{sec:adaptive-search-methods}

Effort control asks how broadly to search.  Method control asks what evidence relation should define
that search.  We decompose one attempt into
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{(\mathcal Q,F,S_r)}_{\text{interpret}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{(R,K,H,S_s)}_{\text{search}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{(B_s,B_{kv},\theta,L,G,M)}_{\text{admit}}.
\label{eq:interpret-search-admit}
\end{equation}
The root method $S_r$ is one of semantic mean-gist, exact token, BM25, approximate token, or static
hybrid search.  The successor method $S_s$ is native semantic, exact new-address, BM25 over the
question/current state, approximate new-address, or state-conditioned hybrid search.  Equation~
\ref{eq:interpret-search-admit} permits, for example, a semantic root followed by an exact address,
or a lexical root followed by native-semantic traversal.  Searching two representations does not
authorize twice as much K/V: $B_s$ and $B_{kv}$ remain separate decisions.

\subsection{Protocol and action contract}

The study imports Paper~2.6's machine-readable action specification and verifies its SHA-256 digest
before use.  Its frozen corpus contains 58 validation and 74 held-out examples across QASPER,
HotpotQA, 2Wiki, and MuSiQue.  All 132 examples have matched two-chunk root rows; 120 have a labelled
two-chunk successor decision, including 68 held-out examples.  Twelve root-only examples remain in
root and cross-dataset analyses but are not assigned a fictitious successor.  One validation example
omits ten of the 25 root/successor pairs; fixed-policy selection maximizes measured cohort coverage
before quality, and missing actions are never imputed.

The serving contract is implemented in
\begin{center}
\path{src/pra_hf/adaptive_search.py}
\end{center}
\texttt{AdaptiveSearchAction} begins at line 81 and carries independent methods around the existing
factorized effort action.  Strict action-spec import begins at line 188; dataset identity, gold
geometry, oracle labels, and answer correctness are rejected from controller features at line 221.
The deterministic method oracle at line 235 is evaluator-only.  Useful-address scoring, targeted
method-delta naming, and operation-specific accounting begin at lines 256, 286, and 314.  The study
runner derives oracle rows at line 221, evaluates policies at line 420, performs leave-one-dataset-out
tests at line 509, and starts bounded method retry at line 639.  These line references make the
interpret/search/admit boundary independently reproducible without coupling it to the paper script.

\subsection{Root and successor method headroom}

Validation selects exact root search.  Its held-out root recall is .066, while selecting the best
measured root method independently per example reaches .104, a .038 absolute diagnostic gap.  The
74 held-out root oracles choose exact for 21 examples, semantic for 19, BM25 for 15, approximate for
11, and hybrid for 8.  No representation has majority support.

Conditioning successor choice on the per-example root oracle reaches .082 recall.  Allowing the
root/successor pair to vary jointly reaches .143, compared with .030 for the validation-selected
fixed pair, BM25 root followed by native-semantic successor.  The 68 successor oracles choose native
semantic 22 times, exact new-address 15, BM25 state 14, approximate new-address 10, and hybrid state
7.  Twenty-two of the 25 possible root-to-successor transitions occur at least once.  The most common
is semantic$\rightarrow$native semantic, with only 9 examples.  Representation choice is therefore
state dependent, but the oracle is an upper bound, not a controller.

\subsection{Generalization, self-query state, and useful addresses}

Leaving each dataset out in turn gives root-method classification accuracy .167 on 2Wiki, .188 on
HotpotQA, .278 on MuSiQue, and .250 on QASPER.  Dataset identity is absent from every feature vector.
These values are too weak to support a general channel controller.  The existing frozen
\texttt{S2\_embed\_last} cache covers only 32 HotpotQA/QASPER identities, all assigned to the Paper-2.6
test split.  We therefore report four-fold identity-grouped out-of-fold classification rather than
mislabeling a fitted-on-test result as held out.  Observable features reach .313 root-method
accuracy, the embedding reaches .438, and their concatenation returns to .313.  This is a small
classification signal, not a measured downstream-quality gain or a reason to replace R0.

\section{Strengthened Request/Reply and a Single Root-State Callback}
\label{sec:request-reply-graph}

The preceding controller selected how many cached facets to use, but not how those facets were
constructed.  That omission would make a callback comparison unfair: a callback with semantic
graphs would have a larger interpretation space than its one-shot baseline.  We therefore first add
the graph action to request/reply, then execute the same root/successor surface with and without one
post-root callback.

\subsection{Five facet modes and one hierarchical representation}

The one-shot action now chooses
\begin{equation}
 F_m\in\{\mathrm{global},\mathrm{syntactic},\mathrm{multiscale},
 \mathrm{graph},\mathrm{syntactic}\rightarrow\mathrm{graph}\}.
\end{equation}
Global uses the final contextual query state.  Syntactic pools deterministic clauses and relation
neighborhoods.  Multiscale pools overlapping windows.  Graph constructs a sparse top-$k$ graph over
contextual query tokens and pools connected components.  The hierarchical mode first partitions
sentences, clauses, delimiters, or fixed regions and then constructs a local semantic graph inside
each region.  It retains the global root, coarse regions, and graph children as distinct scoring
views.

Each tree node records its parent and children, possibly non-contiguous token indices and spans,
embedding and centroid, entities, query-rare tokens, lexical statistics, component density and
weight, and confidence.  This is provenance, not hidden evaluator state.  The graph sees one
already contextualized query pass; it neither re-encodes windows nor inspects evidence labels.
Component minimum and maximum sizes are enforced after clustering and before pooling.

The reusable Paper-2.7 graph primitives are in \path{query_graph.py},
\path{query_graph_cluster.py}, and \path{query_graph_facets.py}.  The adaptive contract begins with
\texttt{GraphFacetConfig} at \path{adaptive_facets.py}:72 and the auditable tree node at line 111.
Graph-region construction begins at line 323; the five-mode builder begins at line 400.
\texttt{AdaptiveSearchAction} at \path{adaptive_search.py}:81 adds facet mode, coarse partition,
graph similarity, threshold, top-$k$, component bounds, fusion, channel profile, and per-facet policy
to the existing independent root/successor action.  Existing callers remain global/fixed by default.

\subsection{Per-facet search methods}

Heterogeneous facets need not share one retrieval representation.  A route now has the form
\begin{equation}
 (q_f,S_{r,f},S_{s,f},K_{r,f},K_{s,f}).
\end{equation}
The fixed baseline applies one pair to every facet.  The type rule sends entity/rare-identifier
facets to exact or BM25 search, relational facets to semantic search, and mixed facets to hybrid
search.  A ridge classifier predicts a root/successor pair from lexical, structural, confidence,
and graph metadata.  The oracle chooses the best measured pair independently per facet and is never
available to serving code.  \texttt{FacetRoute} is defined at \path{facet_routing.py}:31, the linear
selector at line 79, the evaluator-only oracle at line 139, and common routing dispatch at line 168.

\subsection{One bounded \texttt{ROOT\_SELECTED} event}

The serving contract permits exactly one event:
\begin{equation}
 q\rightarrow a_0\rightarrow \mathcal R_1\rightarrow z_{\mathrm{root}}
 \xrightarrow{\texttt{ROOT\_SELECTED}}d_1\rightarrow \mathcal S_1.
 \label{eq:root-callback}
\end{equation}
Here $a_0$ chooses facet and root method, $\mathcal R_1$ is the selected root set,
$z_{\mathrm{root}}$ contains observable post-search state, and $d_1$ chooses one successor method
or conditional graph refinement.  There is no second event and no generation retry.  The callback
cannot inspect dataset identity, gold evidence, or answers.

The state records ordered root identities and scores, top-score gap, candidate entropy, channel
agreement, newly exposed lexical addresses, facet agreement, root dispersion, mean root gist, and
remaining conceptual and K/V budgets.  \texttt{RootState} begins at
\path{src/pra_hf/root_callback.py}:48, the bounded \texttt{RootDecision} at line 145, and the
single-event dispatcher at line 207.  The dispatcher calls a root executor once, invokes
\texttt{on\_root\_selected} once, and passes the decision to a successor executor.  Existing static
execution remains unchanged unless a controller is supplied.

\subsection{Complete measured action surface}

We retain the original 58 validation and 74 test identities rather than creating another post-hoc
split.  Each identity executes nine facet profiles: global with one facet, and structural,
multiscale, graph, and structural-to-graph with two and four requested facets.  Crossing these with
five root methods and five successor methods gives
\begin{equation}
132\times 9\times 5\times 5=29{,}700
\end{equation}
measured actions.  Every action retains two roots, follows two successors, and admits at most four
32-token conceptual chunks.  Thus quality comparisons do not hide broader callback search or K/V
admission.  Root search persists 5,940 reusable states; fitting a new callback does not repeat
retrieval.  Root-state construction is at
\path{experiments/paper3_5_adaptive_pra/request_reply_full_surface.py}:215 and successor execution at
line 280.  The latter caches duplicate root/channel score computations across actions but emits the

Graph construction is not free.  On the test partition, global, structural, multiscale, graph, and

\section{Self-Encoded Query Routing: Letting the Backbone Read the Task Before Allocating Memory}
\label{sec:self-routing}

Instead of immediately adding a separate semantic encoder, the backbone can briefly represent its
own task:
\begin{equation}
 Q\longrightarrow z_Q^{(k)}\longrightarrow \hat a
 \longrightarrow \text{PRA search/admission/consumption}.
\end{equation}
This duplicates only a short question prefill in D0 and can avoid duplication entirely in D1 when
$z_Q^{(k)}$ is an exact prefix of normal execution.  The empirical question is whether that state
predicts sufficient effort better than cheap observable geometry.

\subsection{Double encoding and exact reuse}

The selected embedding state appears before every decoder block and therefore before the earliest
layer-24 PRA consumer.  A fresh D0 prepass costs .020 abstract units, processes 35.2 query-prompt
tokens on average, and takes .015 ms on the measured GPU.  D1 reuses the same normal embedding
state; exact prefix-continuation tests remove recomputation and reduce total cost from 27.20 to
27.18 without changing the .668 routing endpoint.  This reuse result is architectural, not a
quality improvement.

Reuse is invalid after a memory-consuming layer, when ordinary context should precede the query, or
after query reinterpretation changes the selected span.  The 216 controlled wrong-to-correct
reinterpretations therefore require one new encoding of the corrected cue; repeatedly increasing
$F,R,K,H,$ or $B$ around a known-wrong cue is not an equivalent substitute.

The one-index path has exact parity but no consistent CPU speed advantage in this small benchmark;
it is not a fused CUDA kernel.  The fixed-page Python lookup is substantially slower: depending on page size and selection
dispersion, it costs 14--37$\times$ contiguous \texttt{index\_select}.  Pages of 16--128 tokens
waste 15--127 tail slots for the 1,537-token request.  Clustered selection touches 9, 5, 3, and 2
pages; dispersed selection touches 97, 49, 25, and 13.  These numbers explain what a production
page gather should optimize, not how fast that gather will be.

\subsection{Cache and prefetch}

Without prefetch, the best controlled hit rate is .921 at 32 pages; LRU, LFU, and hybrid tie because
the full working set nearly fits.  At 16 pages LFU reaches .896, above LRU's .833, because the
workload contains a stable hot set plus a moving path.  Graph-neighbor prefetch is measured, while
the next-access trace is reported only as an oracle upper bound and never as a deployable predictor.
Useful and wasted prefetch transfers are explicit artifact columns.

\cite{behrouz2025titans} use recurrent, compressed, or external memory.  We preserve this taxonomy
rather than forcing unfair experimental rows.

\section{SDK and Reimplementation Contract}

The runtime is exposed through Python data classes and the \texttt{pra-hf adaptive} CLI.  A caller
can inspect profiles or resolve an automatic plan:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
pra-hf adaptive profiles --output adaptive_effort_profiles.json
pra-hf adaptive plan --mode auto --features request_features.json \
  --controller controller.json --max-retries 2 \
  --max-search-budget 8 --max-active-kv 1024 \
  --latency-budget 1.5 --confidence-threshold 0.35 \
  --trace-output request_plan.json
\end{lstlisting}

Query intent can be supplied as a token span, structured roles, or inferred structurally:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
selector.select(prompt, query_spans=((q_start, q_end),))
selector.select(segments=[
    PromptSegment("query", question),
    PromptSegment("context", logs),
])
selector.select(prompt, policy="structural")
\end{lstlisting}
The selector returns half-open token intervals, confidence, score margin, method, and selected text.
The model adapter can use those intervals to construct facets without assuming that query end equals
prompt end.  Structured segments are preferred in public applications; automatic inference does not
override explicit metadata.

Manual mode uses the same profile representation and accepts explicit query-region, facet, root, neighbor, hop,
conceptual-budget, K/V, threshold, layer, granularity, and materialization overrides.  Overrides are
request-local; they do not silently redefine the validated global ladder.

An independent implementation needs five invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Separate conceptual and physical budgets in the public request and trace schema.
\item Reject evaluator labels before feature vectorization; do not merely promise not to use them.
\item Preserve prior selected identities and pages when escalating, and account for regenerated
tokens separately.
\item Label every timing as component, end-to-end, measured, or extrapolated.  A Python microbenchmark
cannot become a production throughput claim by omission.
\item Keep query-role discovery separate from faceting, and preserve explicit caller spans or roles
through retries unless the caller permits reinterpretation.
\end{enumerate}

The relevant implementation is compact.  \path{adaptive_runtime.py} defines profiles, feature
validation, learned and hand controllers, stop policy, retries, consistency, and calibration.
\path{query_regions.py} defines structured segments, explicit spans, structural discovery,
multi-region selection, and cue reinterpretation.  \path{effort_router.py} defines finite action
spaces, independent categorical heads, a cached semantic-input contract, and autoregressive heads.
\path{factorized_control.py} defines valid independent actions, exact component accounting,
token-weighted evidence metrics, Pareto dominance, allocation outcomes, and retry action names.
\path{factorized_study.py} executes the frozen lattice and emits the oracle, router, and retry
artifacts.  \path{self_router.py} adds query-span pooling over tensors $[B,T,\ldots]$, exact
native Q/K capture from the layer-normalized residual stream, validation-only projection, grouped
action decoding, prefill accounting, and Qwen prefix continuation.  Q and K are reshaped to
$[B,T,H,d_h]$ and passed through the layer's own \texttt{q\_norm}/\texttt{k\_norm} before question-span
pooling, matching Qwen's pre-RoPE attention geometry rather than exposing raw projection outputs.
The precompute script stores
only pooled vectors; \path{self_router_study.py} owns oracle labels, grouped CV, target codebooks,
and plots.  Physical-runtime implementation remains behind the control/execution boundary.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What adaptive control accomplished}

The profile experiment first appeared to make HotpotQA uniformly high effort: 31 of 35 held-out units
needed E2.  Factorization changes that interpretation.  Every Hotpot unit is solved at lower mean
cost because most need only one facet, low $K$, and at most one hop; the expensive requirement is
usually concentrated in roots or total parent budget.  Coherent profiles are robust, but they pay
for controls that an individual query does not require.

QASPER remains the more heterogeneous workload.  Its factorized oracle uses $H=0$ on 96.7\% of
held-out units and obtains a small quality gain over the profile lattice.  These oracle results
justify fine-grained actions in principle.  The learned-router results deny the stronger claim that
the present controller can allocate them safely: every R1--R3A variant loses quality through
under-allocation.  R0 therefore remains the supported initial controller despite measurable
quantization regret.

Retry is similarly conditional.  A bounded evaluator-side correction closes 19 of 24 initial
failures, and eight corrections change only roots or facets.  The remaining solvable failures need
compound changes.  This establishes a target for a future diagnostic classifier, not an achieved
online correction policy.  Universal cheap-first escalation remains inferior to direct prediction.

The add-on studies sharpen what escalation means.  Failure may require a different cue rather than
more roots under the same misplaced cue.  Explicit roles and spans are both cheaper and more reliable
than discovering role from raw serialization, so applications should expose them when possible.
Automatic structural reinterpretation is a bounded fallback.  Query-region breadth has the same
two-sided character as other PRA controls: it can recover distributed instructions, but it can also
dilute the objective and multiply facets, roots, and comparisons.

They also provide a controller-complexity stop.  Independent and autoregressive heads now receive
genuine factorized targets, yet neither improves the quality/cost frontier.  The result is no longer
an artifact of copying E0/E1/E2 labels.  Self-encoding then removes the query-representation
ambiguity without changing that result: validation-selected embedding-last and contextual hidden
states both fall below observables on held-out units.  The external-encoder gate therefore remains
closed.
A compact pretrained encoder is justified only after a larger grouped natural cohort establishes
semantic headroom that this cohort does not.

\subsection{Cognitive interpretation}

The tested computational principle is modest:
\begin{quote}
encode task $\rightarrow$ allocate effort $\rightarrow$ execute $\rightarrow$ detect uncertainty
$\rightarrow$ selective correction $\rightarrow$ stop.
\end{quote}
Natural cognitive systems plausibly vary retrieval cues and deliberation under uncertainty, but
the present controller is not a biological model.  Its scientific content is the measured frontier
between quality and expected resource use.

\subsection{Program handoff}

Paper~3.5 ends at an adaptive initial action, observable sufficiency evidence, and one bounded
correction.  Kernel compilation, indexed-search crossover, physical memory hierarchy, batching,
and concurrency are Paper~5.5 questions.  First-class vLLM logical blocks, asynchronous memory
movement, and scheduler awareness are Paper~6 questions.  This boundary keeps control quality from
being conflated with a Python prototype's throughput.

\section{Limitations}

The adaptive quality endpoint is evidence-chain completion from frozen traces, not a live generated
answer.  Output confidence comes from a separate controlled model, so a combined routing/output
controller remains untested.  The held-out adaptive cohort contains only 65 example--seed units and
inherits Paper-2.5's model and dataset choices.

The supported learned controller is the R0 profile selector.  Factorized R1--R3A use independently
measured targets, but the cohort is too small to establish reliable natural parameter interactions.
R2 uses actual questions through a dependency-free signed-hash encoder rather than a pretrained NLP
encoder.  The output calibration cohort is small and imbalanced.  Semantic consistency uses token
F1 and can miss paraphrase and contradiction.

The self-router sweep contains 32 unique questions from HotpotQA and QASPER and one frozen
Qwen3-0.6B backbone.  Repeated model seeds provide routing variation but not additional linguistic
identities.  Validation-selected embedding-last does not generalize to held-out units, while the
numerical held-out maximum for embedding mean was not validation-selected and is not a confirmed
gain.  The
contextual condition processes an ordinary truncated source prompt as a diagnostic; it is not the
normal PRA execution path.  2Wiki, MuSiQue, learned attention pooling, another model family, and an
external encoder remain untested because they lack this add-on's frozen factorized surface or fail
the staged expansion rule.  CUDA timings are from one GTX~950M and are not deployment latency claims.

Query-region evaluation remains deterministic.  Semi-natural email, code, log, URL, tool-output, and
conversation fixtures broaden surface form but are not sampled from a natural prompt distribution.
The adversarial structural failure shows that ambiguous role resolution remains open.  Tokenizer-
specific span mapping and a lightweight learned span scorer remain untested.

The factorized endpoint is frozen-score evidence-chain recovery, not generated-answer quality.
The original factorized surface ranks existing cached facets.  Section~\ref{sec:request-reply-graph}
adds parameter-free facet construction from frozen query states, but it is not a trained semantic
interpreter.  Its first 45/29 analysis is post-hoc within the prior test cohort; the subsequent
full-surface callback study restores the original 58/74 partition.  Search-order
admission does not exploit $B_s>B_{kv}$ in any held-out minimum-cost oracle.  The targeted correction
audit uses evaluator success to choose an action and must not be read as an oracle-free retry result.
Five QASPER units remain unsolved throughout the measured lattice.

The search-method extension inherits Paper~2.6's small matched-budget discovery corpus and exhaustive
Python implementations.  It contains 74 held-out root identities but only 68 with successor labels;
complete broad-span recovery is zero at the four-chunk request budget.  The per-example method oracle
uses evaluator labels, and the 32-identity factorized join is a composition of two measured surfaces,
not a joint execution.  Leave-one-dataset-out method accuracy is weak.  The useful-address gate
abstains on every held-out example, and the learned retry action accuracy is .221.  The self-query
method comparison is four-fold out-of-fold because all overlapping cache identities belong to the
Paper-2.6 test split; it is not a validation-frozen downstream result.  Query-region$\times$method
interaction is unidentifiable because only two examples use the minority layout.  Unlike method
operations are intentionally not collapsed into one systems-cost scalar.

The first graph-facet replay measures root discovery only and uses a post-hoc 45/29 split.  The newer
29,700-action study repairs both limitations for one fixed two-root/two-successor budget, but it does
not cross hop depth, effort, per-facet routing, or variable physical admission.  Hierarchical overlap
counts coarse parent/child reuse; global hard-community overlap is zero by construction.  Prototype
graph timings include Python control flow and are not serving measurements.  The single callback is
tested with small ridge and threshold controllers over 58 validation identities; B7 is an evaluator
oracle, not a deployable policy.  The eight-example decode cohort verifies native-K/V propagation but
is too small for dataset-level answer-quality claims.

Finally, no full PRA backbone is trained here.  Learned consumer adaptation and full-weight scaling
are deferred to Paper~4.

\section{Conclusion}

PRA effort is better represented by separable interpretation, search, and admission controls than by
one diagonal profile lattice.  At matched complete-chain quality, factorization reduces mean held-out
HotpotQA cost from 34.18 to 19.98 and matched QASPER cost from 16.11 to 8.95.  The result resolves the
main scientific question: profile quantization hides useful low-cost combinations.

The control problem is not solved.  R1--R3A reduce nominal cost by under-allocating and remain below
R0 quality, even with factorized targets.  Signed hashing, deep self-state, native Q/K, and the
validation-selected contextual upper bound do not reverse that result.  R0 stays the SDK default.
One bounded oracle corrective action exposes substantial retry headroom, but compound changes and
five unsolved QASPER units prevent a stronger online claim.

Candidate normalization explains where that headroom comes from.  QASPER's factorized oracle reaches
.917 evidence-parent recall and .796 precision while admitting 10.9\% of candidates, compared with
.750, .450, and 18.2\% for R0.  Retry reaches the oracle recall but admits 19.0\% and has lower
precision.  HotpotQA retry raises recall to .948 at a denser 75.6\% admission fraction.  Thus adaptive
control can expose sparse working sets, especially on QASPER, but the learned controller has not yet
found them reliably.

Retrieval representation adds another genuine but unresolved control surface.  Exact root search
selected on validation reaches .066 recall, while the root oracle reaches .104.  At the two-stage
budget, the fixed BM25$\rightarrow$native-semantic pair reaches .030, the interpretable cascade .048,
the learned joint selector .032, and the joint method oracle .143.  Root and successor methods should
therefore remain independent actions, but a validation-fixed pair remains the conservative default
until selection improves.  C0 is the strongest measured deployable cascade, not a universal policy:
it loses to the fixed pair on QASPER and MuSiQue.  Always switching methods hurts; learned targeted
retry raises recall only from .032 to .037.

Graph faceting completes the capability side of that baseline without solving prediction.  The
exploratory post-hoc split shows per-facet headroom, but the stronger full-surface result is
conservative: B1 and B2 both reach about .180 recall, while B3--B5 reach .167--.168.  The transparent
threshold policy reaches .188 but does not beat the .205 validation-fixed global policy.  B6 and B7
are identical at .365, so this action surface provides no stage-wise oracle gain.  The callback API
is useful as an experimental boundary and trace format, not as the SDK default.  A future callback
iteration needs different supervision, state, or legal actions; more capacity on the same labels is
not the next justified step.

Adaptive PRA must also decide what the query is.  Explicit roles remain preferred; structural and
recent interpretations should compete under confidence because either can fail under serialization
or decoys.  The contribution is therefore a measured control surface, a conservative default, and a
bounded retry endpoint.  The runtime now follows the six-stage cycle in
Equation~\ref{eq:interpret-search-admit}, followed by evaluation and targeted retry.  A self-prefill
captured before layer 24 can be reused exactly, which makes
future semantic control cheaper, but the present controller does not earn that added complexity.
Making those decisions fast belongs to Paper~5.5; making them native to vLLM belongs to Paper~6.

\section{Decision and Accounting Details}

The learned controller uses standardized ridge least squares against one-hot E0/E1/E2 targets.
The failure controller uses the same estimator with success/failure targets.  Validation selects a
.3 maximum predicted-failure threshold for retry stopping.  AUROC is computed by exact pairwise
ranking, AUPRC by average precision over descending risk, ECE with ten equal-width bins, and Brier
score as mean squared probability error.

Risk--coverage sorts examples from lowest to highest predicted failure.  At coverage $c$, selective
risk is the failure fraction among the lowest-risk $cN$ observations.  We retain every point in the
calibration JSON rather than reading values back from a rendered figure.

Search comparisons count query--key dot products.  K/V bytes count both keys and values.  Page
fragmentation is allocated page slots minus valid tokens.  Batch allocation counts padded or page
rounded token slots.  CPU benchmark bytes are physical tensor sizes; HBM/request is an explicitly
marked extrapolation from token count, dimension, dtype, and K/V multiplicity.

For factorized control, profile quantization regret is defined only when both the profile and
factorized action spaces contain a complete-chain configuration:
\begin{equation}
 R_{\mathrm{quantization}}=C_{\mathrm{profile\ oracle}}-
 C_{\mathrm{factorized\ oracle}}.
\end{equation}
Under-allocation means the selected action misses a chain recovered by the factorized oracle.
Over-allocation means it matches oracle quality at higher cost.  A failed unit is never counted as a
cost saving.  Held-out oracle labels are used only for evaluation and corrective-action upper bounds.

For self-routing, each grouped-CV fold fits centering and PCA components on unique training
identities only, then applies the frozen transform to its validation identities.  Final held-out
models refit on all validation identities.  Query-prefill cost is always added to the selected
retrieval action unless the exact prefix state is reused.  Parameter-label accuracy is diagnostic;
Tables~\ref{tab:self-representations} and~\ref{tab:self-targets} report downstream chain quality,
under-allocation, and total cost instead.  The source-plus-query condition pays all 256 ordinary
context tokens and is never presented as a free in-flight signal.
