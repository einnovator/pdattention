\title{From Sparse Selection to Physical Savings:\\
A Unified Runtime and Measurement Contract for Progressive Retrieval Attention}
\author{PRA Research Program}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We ask how Progressive Retrieval Attention (PRA) can become a portable runtime
abstraction without confusing a small logical selection with a systems-level
saving. We organize deployment into three depths: Selected Context serializes
chosen evidence as visible text (E0); Native Memory supplies selected
model-native key/value state outside the visible prompt (E2); and Native
Serving gives the engine scheduler direct ownership of placement and reuse
(E3). The implementation unifies authenticated resources, typed records,
tasks, sessions, routing, physical materialization, storage lifecycle, engine
capability negotiation, and evidence receipts behind one runtime and gateway.
The contract keeps ordinary prefix K/V distinct from query-addressed PRA state
and falls back to E0 when a deeper capability is unavailable.

The evaluation separates quality, context, and economics. On frozen routed
evidence, MLX-LM, SGLang-MLX, and vLLM-Metal remove 90.6--93.0\% of visible
prompt tokens and preserve 6,258/6,258 paired outputs across 149 questions per
engine. Lossless WARM storage preserves 265/265 outputs over 17
model--dataset cohorts and survives restart, whereas int8 COLD storage is exact
in only 61/265 sequences and remains opt-in. Qwen3-32B reaches a warm E2/E0
cost ratio of 1.011 and a cold usable-context ratio of 0.976 on Apple Silicon.
A scheduler-invisible CUDA vLLM prototype preserves 128/128 outputs in each of
FULL, E0, E2-HOT, and E2-WARM, but E0 sustains 56.3 requests/s versus 45.5 and
42.4 for the two native modes. Thus semantic parity and scheduler invisibility
do not yet imply an economic win.
On a 48~GiB M4 Pro, a Qwen3.5-27B MLX 4-bit checkpoint runs at 31.1 decode
tokens/s and fits in 15.4~GiB at smoke scale. At a 20\% selected-context budget,
it removes 77--79\% of visible prompt tokens and reduces mean TTFT by 76--79\%
on 32 held-out QASPER and HotpotQA questions. Its learned router helps QASPER
but hurts HotpotQA, and detached native K/V is unavailable because 48 of 64
layers require DeltaNet recurrent state beyond K/V.

Using MultiHop-RAG only as a locked engine workload, MLX-LM contiguous Native
Memory preserves every selector-frozen output. Retained K/V reduces warm total
latency from 3.031 to 0.638~s at 2K and from 6.121 to 0.799~s at 4K, while cold
native execution remains 1.8--3.3\% slower across Qwen3-4B/8B. Independently
cached chunks reduce runtime but change outputs, so they remain unqualified.
Papers~3.2 and~3.3 own the corresponding retrieval and selector conclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

PRA changes what a model must keep logically available.  A long-lived resource
may remain addressable through a compact routing representation while only a
small selected part of its native K/V participates in one attention operation.
That distinction is algorithmically useful, but it leaves a systems question
unanswered.  A selected set can be small while the runtime still stores every
detail tensor in HBM, launches many tiny gathers, copies the same bytes
repeatedly, constructs temporary concatenations, or serializes requests around
Python control flow.  In that case sparsity exists in a trace but not in the
resource bill.

This paper asks how to turn PRA into a portable inference primitive before a
serving engine becomes semantically aware of PRA memory.  The immediate task is
not to design a new scheduler.  It is to define an execution boundary that can
be measured, optimized, and later replaced without changing the meaning of a
selected reference.

Three previously separate interfaces must meet at that boundary.  The Paper 2
SDK supplies \texttt{PRAForCausalLM}, model-family injection, routing, and
generation.  Paper 4 supplies authenticated resolvers and the cold, warm, and
hot external-memory lifecycle.  Paper 6.5 supplies stable typed identities,
callable-derived tool schemas, declarative skills, lazy disclosure, and
host-authorized tool execution. Paper 7 supplies type-aware compact views,
scoped lossless backing, size-gated native indexing, lazy selected-region
encoding, selective materialization, and cursors for large results. Paper 8
supplies versioned task state, task-scoped record admission,
and durable user/session resolution. We retain these responsibilities and place one orchestration facade
above them.  This is integration by contract rather than by collapsing distinct
policies.

The contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a unified runtime contract joining model memory, authenticated resources,
typed capabilities and results, durable tasks and sessions, and independently
authorized tool execution without collapsing their policy boundaries;
\item a physical K/V contract that preserves grouped-query heads, separates
selection sparsity, materialization width, and consumer-layer coverage, merges
intervals before budgeting, and accounts for transfers, temporary bytes, and
bounded cache reuse;
\item a corrected source-relative position and request-lifetime implementation,
with exact disabled behavior and measured full-native semantic gates on Qwen
and Llama plus a localized Gemma global/local topology limitation;
\item an engine-neutral, engine-type-aware gateway with independent PRA and
ordinary cache/session capabilities, explicit lifecycle, append-only G10
placement, message/resource deltas, affinity hints, and non-sensitive session
inspection, while advertising phase selection separately from unsupported
scheduler hints;
\item lazy tools and skills, size-adaptive typed-result indexing, task-scoped
acquisition, external-agent bridges, and a user-facing runtime/agent interface;
and
\item reproducible eager measurements and explicit negative gates for compilation,
modern-GPU transfer overlap, and native serving-engine integration.
\end{itemize}

\section{PRA Runtime Abstraction}

\subsection{Logical and physical memory are different quantities}

Consider $L$ decoder layers.  Each reference has $T$ tokens, $H_{kv}$ physical
key/value heads, head width $D$, and $b$ bytes per scalar.  Storing complete K/V
for the reference requires
\begin{equation}
B_{\mathrm{detail}} = 2 L H_{kv} T D b,
\end{equation}
where the factor two accounts for keys and values.  If routing selects $S$
unique tokens, the active payload is
\begin{equation}
B_{\mathrm{active}} = 2 L_{c} H_{kv} S D b,
\end{equation}
where $L_c$ is the number of memory-consuming layers.  The ratio
$B_{\mathrm{active}}/B_{\mathrm{detail}}$ is a logical opportunity.  It is not
peak HBM.  Peak HBM also includes model weights, local K/V, routing indices,
allocator slack, transfer staging, and temporary packed tensors.

Grouped-query and multi-query attention make the distinction especially
important.  Warm storage should preserve $H_{kv}$ physical heads.  Expanding
them to the larger number of query heads before materialization duplicates
memory without adding information.  The runtime therefore represents every
native tensor as
\begin{equation}
K,V \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times H_{kv} \times T \times D}
\end{equation}
and leaves head expansion to the model-family attention adapter.

These equations require four layer sets, not one overloaded set of ``PRA
layers.'' Let $L_A$ store compact addresses, $L_{KV}$ store detailed native
K/V, $L_R$ perform request-time routing, and $L_C$ consume selected memory.
The runtime enforces
\begin{equation}
L_C \subseteq L_{KV}, \qquad
L_R \subseteq L_A \quad \text{for layer-native routing}.
\end{equation}
The persistent detail term is therefore


\begin{equation}
B_{\mathrm{detail}} = 2 |L_{KV}| H_{kv} T D b,
\end{equation}
whereas one request pays active attention over $|L_C|S$ layer-token states.
Address layers may be broader than detail-K/V layers when compact search is
useful at several depths. Production defaults do the opposite kind of
conservatism: detail K/V is encoded only for the resolved consumer profile,
unless profile switching or calibration explicitly requests a union.

\subsection{Latency is a sum of visible stages}

For one request, PRA overhead is not a single routing timer.  We decompose it as
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{PRA}} = t_{\mathrm{lookup}} + t_{\mathrm{graph}} +
t_{\mathrm{plan}} + t_{\mathrm{gather}} + t_{\mathrm{transfer}} +
t_{\mathrm{pack}} + t_{\mathrm{position}} + t_{\mathrm{metadata}}.
\end{equation}
Generation and attention follow this preparation.  Cold requests also pay
source fetch and native encoding; compiled paths pay graph capture and code
generation.  Reporting only warm attention can therefore reverse the practical
conclusion.

\subsection{One facade, separate authorities}

component interchangeable in meaning.  It gives them one lifecycle and one
inspection surface while retaining their ownership boundaries.

The public configuration has two layers.  \texttt{PRAConfig} specifies
semantic behavior and all four model-layer roles: \texttt{address\_layers},
\texttt{detail\_kv\_layers}, \texttt{routing\_layers}, and
\texttt{consumption\_layers}, plus chunking, closure mode, and materialization
budget. \texttt{PRARuntimeConfig} specifies physical behavior:
backend, compilation mode, K/V layout, page size, cache bounds, prefetch policy,
and timing synchronization.  Both serialize to a versioned JSON artifact.  A
runtime optimization is valid only when changing the second layer preserves
selected identities and output parity under fixed model semantics.

Layer selections use topology-normalized forms: all layers, last $n$, last
fraction, evenly spaced $n$, or an explicit list. A versioned registry resolves
request override, workload/model profile, family balanced fallback, then the
all-eligible correctness reference. The named objectives are
\texttt{reference\_correctness}, \texttt{quality\_max}, \texttt{balanced}, and
\texttt{economy}; the resolved trace records model ID and revision, workload,
materialization class, physical layer lists, profile source, and registry
version. These are internal optimization objectives. The product registry
labels smoke-only realizations of \texttt{quality\_max} as
\QualityMaxCandidate. A profile name is not portable evidence by
itself.

Address and detail construction have independent lifecycle states
(\texttt{NOT\_BUILT}, \texttt{PARTIAL}, \texttt{BUILT}, \texttt{SKIPPED}, or
\texttt{DEFERRED}). The detail policy is minimal, profile union, all layers, or
explicit. The address policy is routing-only, all candidate routing layers,
external-only, or explicit. If a new consumer requires absent detail K/V, the
runtime must re-encode missing layers, fail, or follow an explicitly configured
profile downgrade. It never silently substitutes a shallower profile.

\subsection{Engine instances and runtime model identity}

PRA distinguishes engine-instance identity from loaded-model identity. One
engine process may expose one or more loaded models. Model-specific PRA
state---including bundle, profile, qualification, session state, storage, and
native memory---is scoped by a stable runtime-model identity and model
fingerprint. Engines supporting only one base model use the same contract with
a single \texttt{default} loaded model. This keeps multi-model behavior in the
management container rather than turning the semantic runtime for one model
into a fleet manager.

Internally, the management provider maps each \texttt{runtime\_model\_id} to a
small runtime-state record containing the observed model descriptor, storage
manager, session source, capabilities, and effective configuration. The old
single-model constructor is normalized to one such record. Canonical source
records may be shared across models, but derived native identity includes the
model fingerprint together with resource identity, version, selected span or
representation, and layout. Model-native K/V can therefore neither cross a
model switch nor survive an unload as an apparently compatible prefix.

The Registry retains one engine instance with a \texttt{models[]} observation
and accepts either the legacy singular desired fields or a
\texttt{desired\_models[]} list. Drift is matched by runtime identity and
reported both per model and in aggregate. The gateway similarly routes to the
pair \((\textit{instance},\textit{runtime model})\), which distinguishes one
model replicated on several engines from several models resident behind one
upstream. Dynamic load and unload are optional capabilities; a single-model
runtime returns \texttt{NOT\_APPLICABLE}, \texttt{ACTION\_NOT\_SUPPORTED}, or
\texttt{RESTART\_REQUIRED} rather than pretending that process replacement is
an in-process model transition.

\subsection{A multi-axis execution policy}

PRA does not imply one routing frequency. We represent one request with four
orthogonal choices. The \emph{selection stage} is request, prefill/completion
phase, or token. The \emph{layer scope} is shared logical identities or an
independent selection per active layer. The \emph{materialization scope} is
request, phase, layer, or token. The \emph{residency policy} retains a payload
for that lifetime or releases it after one layer window. Shared identities never
mean shared tensors: chunk $C_7$ resolves to the native K/V independently
encoded at each consuming layer.

The global default is request/shared/request/keep. A model default can override
individual fields, and a request override takes final precedence. The resolved
policy records field-level provenance. Unsupported combinations raise before
generation; there is no implicit downgrade. The engine-neutral
\texttt{PRASelectionPlan} contains stable URI/chunk identities and row-local
spans, never tensors. A layer resolver maps that plan to native payloads, and a
separate materialization manager applies physical lifetime and residency.

The HF reference path preserves the old high-level request/shared behavior and
the old low-level token/per-layer behavior. It adds request/per-layer by
capturing all active layers in one memory-disabled prompt pass, routing each
once, and freezing each result. It adds token/shared through a request-owned
bridge: layers before the designated routing layer receive no current-token PRA
memory; that layer selects once, and later layers resolve the same identities
to their own K/V. Phase/shared execution uses a cache-free routing probe at the
first active PRA layer, seeds the shared logical plan, and then performs one
cache-producing prefill. Decode reselects once at the same boundary and reuses
the resulting plan. Requiring the routing layer to be the first active consumer
prevents an earlier layer from entering the cache with a different memory view.

\subsection{Stable resources and authenticated sessions}

A stable URI is the common identity for text memory, native K/V, tools, and
typed observations.  Paper 4's \texttt{PRASession} retains tenant, user,
session, cache scope, admitted resources, and warm/hot handles.  Credentials
remain opaque callbacks passed only to resolvers; they are absent from prompts,
snapshots, and artifacts.  Cache presence never grants authorization.

\subsection{Durable logical sessions and task scope}

An interactive agent needs continuity longer than one model process. The
runtime therefore separates logical \texttt{AgentSessionState} from physical
\texttt{PRASession}. Logical state is resolved by user and session identity and
contains an ordered typed-record stream, a task descriptor, and an idempotent
event log. Physical state owns admitted resources, native K/V, and result-store
handles. Closing it releases accelerator and resolver state without deleting
the conversation or task graph.

\texttt{SessionService} is the persistence boundary. Its in-memory
implementation provides thread-safe ephemeral operation. Its local
implementation hashes path identities, writes versioned JSON through atomic
replacement, and rejects stale optimistic versions. A database can implement
the same create, resolve, save, list, and delete contract without changing the
model path.

Task events create, link, activate, block, resume, complete, or cancel nodes in
a validated DAG. Typed records carry producing-task, consuming-task, relation,
and event-sequence provenance. Before ordinary PRA ranking,
\texttt{TaskScopeSelector} admits the active task, its structural closure, or a
bounded adaptive neighborhood. Scope controls which records may compete;
record and chunk routing still control which admitted detail is materialized.

\subsection{Agent and toolset product surface}

\texttt{Toolset} couples a callable to its generated schema, namespace, tenant,
tags, and declared side-effect class. Bundles compose through the existing
\texttt{CapabilitySDK}. The built-in local bundle provides file listing,
bounded reads and search, exact writes and replacements, Git status, and
workspace-rooted command execution. Path operations reject traversal outside
the configured workspace. Shell commands remain write-class actions:
workspace selection is not a security sandbox.

\texttt{PRAAgent} assembles model, capability discovery, lazy skill folders,
safe execution, compact results, durable sessions, and task scope. A turn
persists the user record, selects task-relevant records, discloses a bounded
tool palette, generates, validates at most a configured number of tool rounds,
stores compact output with exact-backing provenance, and persists the final
assistant record. The terminal adds resumable sessions and slash commands for
tasks, context, and tools. Write and destructive calls are denied by default
and can receive one host confirmation; disclosure alone never grants authority.

Paper 6.5's tool resources enter through the same runtime but a separate
decision path.  Discovery can identify a tool.  Disclosure can expose its
schema.  A model can propose a call.  Only \texttt{SafeToolExecutor}, given a
request-scoped \texttt{ExecutionAuthorization}, can validate identity,
arguments, write authority, and destructive authority.  Its result becomes a
typed observation resource with producer and call provenance.  This separation
prevents a high retrieval score from turning into ambient execution privilege.

\subsection{Lazy tools and skills}

Paper 6.5's latest capability contract is now part of the facade rather than an
adjacent experiment.  \texttt{AgentConfig} accepts ordinary Python callables,
explicit declarative \texttt{Skill} objects, and a parent directory containing
OpenAI- or Anthropic-style skill folders.  Callables are inspected into stable,
provider-neutral schemas.  Folder loaders parse declarative metadata and
instructions but never execute bundled scripts.

The default is a two-stage model-side lifecycle.  Discovery and Phase A encode
only compact selection views under count and token budgets.  Once the model or
host chooses a stable record ID, \texttt{activate\_capability} activates the
complete tool schema or skill instructions from immutable local backing.  It
does not rerun semantic discovery.  Encoded views may be cached, while active
bytes and resident cached bytes remain separate counters.  Eager selection or
full-view encoding is an explicit policy override.

\subsection{Compact typed result backing}

A successful tool output need not return as one flat prompt block.  Every open
\texttt{PRASession} now owns an \texttt{AdaptiveContextRuntime} with an exact,
hash-verified backing store scoped by tenant and session.  The runtime infers
tabular, log, graph, terminal, or generic structured shape for tool and API
results.  Type-specific compactors retain schema, counts, statistics, errors,
and bounded representative units while lexical, entity, rare-term, schema, and
optional summary addresses remain retrieval-only.

The public operations preserve stable record identity: compact inspection,
address search, full or selected materialization, and bounded cursor actions.
\texttt{execute\_tool\_and\_record} joins authorized execution to this record
lifecycle while retaining producer, call, and observation provenance.  Closing
an ephemeral session removes its backing bytes.

Paper 7's final result changes the product default. Native PRA is appropriate
for typed backing only while its ingestion and resident-state cost stays inside
an explicit budget. Above that budget, the record must remain recoverable
through cheaper addresses, search, cursors, and bounded lazy native encoding;
it must not be truncated and labeled as a complete native index.

Accordingly, \texttt{ContextPolicy} exposes
\texttt{max\_native\_index\_tokens} and
\texttt{max\_native\_index\_bytes}, plus per-\texttt{RecordType}
overrides. The byte gate can reject an oversized descriptor before tokenization;
the token gate more directly bounds model work when token accounting is
available. The reference defaults are 4,096 tokens and 65,536 bytes, but these
are deployment policy values rather than claimed universal optima.

Every record has one auditable native-index lifecycle state:
\begin{center}
\small\ttfamily
NOT\_REQUESTED\quad BUILT\quad SKIPPED\_SIZE\_LIMIT\quad DEFERRED
\end{center}
The ordinary Hugging Face loader automatically prepares in-budget records.
\texttt{BUILT} means complete backing passed through the production PRA encoder.
\texttt{SKIPPED\_SIZE\_LIMIT} preserves compact and cheap-index recovery.
\texttt{DEFERRED} is an explicit synchronous lifecycle decision, not a claim of
background work. After a cheap selector identifies a bounded row, field, line,
item, or chunk, \texttt{encode\_result\_region\_native} authorizes that exact
region and creates native K/V only for it. Teardown removes compact, full, and
lazy-region references. A shared multi-tenant model cache is not claimed safe
without engine-level partitioning.

\subsection{User workflow}

The supported workflow is deliberately small:

\begin{verbatim}
agent = AgentConfig(tools=(lookup,), skills_path="./skills")
context = ContextPolicy(
    max_native_index_tokens=4096,
    max_native_index_bytes=65536,
    record_policies={DB_RESULT: TypeContextPolicy(...)})
runtime = PRARuntime.from_pretrained(
    model_id, runtime_config=config,
    agent_config=agent, context_policy=context)
session = runtime.open_session(...)
palette = runtime.activate_capability_candidates(record_ids)
runtime.activate_capability(selected_id)
result = runtime.execute_tool_and_record(..., session=session)
audit = runtime.result_native_index_audit(
    session, result.record.record_id)
if audit.native_index_state == SKIPPED_SIZE_LIMIT:
    detail = runtime.encode_result_region_native(
        session, result.record.record_id, {"rows": [20, 30]})
trace = runtime.inspect()
\end{verbatim}

The same facade still accepts direct references, an
\texttt{ExternalMemoryManager}, a custom discovery engine, and a safe executor.
The executed notebook demonstrates model memory, physical materialization,
lazy capabilities, compact results, cursors, size-gated native result routing,
and selected-region native promotion
with an offline Hugging Face model and pure in-memory tools.

\section{Physical Materialization}

\subsection{Planning before copying}

Routing produces logical half-open intervals
$I_i=(u_i,\ell_i,a_i,b_i)$, naming URI $u_i$, layer $\ell_i$, and token range
$[a_i,b_i)$.  The planner groups intervals by URI and layer, sorts them, merges
overlaps, and then applies a global token budget.  If two requested intervals
overlap, their common tokens are copied once.  The trace reports requested,
unique, and dropped token counts, making deduplication and budget truncation
auditable.

The portable materializer slices source tensors, transfers selected slices when
needed, and packs them by consumption layer.  It reports output bytes, transfer
bytes, and temporary bytes.  It does not infer peak HBM from these values; CUDA
profiling records peak allocator state separately.

\subsection{Native-reference semantic gate}

Reference K/V is captured at source-relative positions. The direct query must
therefore begin strictly after every source key that it may attend to. For
independent records $r$ with source lengths $L_r$, the runtime uses
\begin{equation}
p_{q,j}=\max_r L_r+j,
\end{equation}
not $j$ and not $\sum_r L_r+j$. The maximum avoids query/source position
collisions while preserving each record's independent coordinate system.
Earlier code started $p_{q,0}=0$, which invalidated full-reference RoPE parity.
This was a runtime bug, not a routing or training deficiency.

The corrected path enters selected native K/V and local K/V into the same
family-native softmax. It retains grouped-query K/V heads physically, preserves
the model's scaling and causal mask, and keeps the immutable materialization
plan active through prefill and every decode call. Compact telemetry records
query width, local-cache width, and active native width without retaining
attention tensors.

The storage fingerprint now distinguishes fixed \texttt{post\_rope} keys from
optional \texttt{pre\_rope} keys. A pre-RoPE entry carries a contract over the
model revision, every layer's exact frequency tensor and scaling policy, RoPE
dimensions, and rotation layout. At request time an adapter may apply the
selected packed positions before exposing the entry to attention. Contract
mismatch invalidates the derived cache. Post-RoPE remains the default because
it avoids request-time rotation when positions are fixed; pre-RoPE is an
experimental composition facility, not a claim that rotation alone restores
the causal history missing from independently encoded records.

Papers~3.2 and~3.3 own the detailed document-selection, positional-rebinding,
and answer-quality studies.  Paper~4.5 imports only their runtime decision:
independently encoded pre-RoPE records remain
\texttt{CALIBRATION\_PENDING}.  An engine adapter must qualify its RoPE contract
and numerical path, and that qualification does not recreate cross-record
causal contextualization.

The bug fix closes the semantic blocker but does not make every sparse geometry

\subsection{Cross-model semantic reference gate}

We next apply one architecture-neutral protocol to three frozen pretrained
models. The exact checkpoints are Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B revision
\texttt{c1899de2}, \texttt{unsloth/Llama-3.2-1B} revision \texttt{9535bd9b},
and \texttt{google/gemma-3-1b-it} revision \texttt{dcc83ea8}. The official
Meta Llama revision \texttt{4e20de36} remained license-gated to the available
account; the experiment therefore uses a public weight mirror and records that
substitution rather than implying that the official repository was tested.
All runs use eager FP16 CUDA on the GTX 950M, one model per process.

Before the semantic cases, each model must preserve logits, hidden states,
greedy generation, and the complete dynamic cache exactly with PRA disabled.
Reference registration must retain native K/V-head count and head width without
query-head expansion, preserve source-relative positions, and remain active for
two explicit decode calls. The four measured conditions are a visible prefix,
the same complete reference as native K/V, an explicitly selected complete
record, and a family-declared sparse layer profile. Three one-token
continuations test a capital, color, and numeric code. This small diagnostic
localizes integration failures; it is not an answer-quality benchmark.

The executable qualification path fixes the candidate chunks once, compares
generic cosine with a five-seed evidence-supervised router, and replays each

router gain cannot be mistaken for a transport gain, and native parity is
measured against the exact selected-text twin rather than an independently
retrieved context.

\subsection{Cross-family learned-router qualification}

Structural compatibility does not answer whether an extra routing adapter is
useful. We therefore compare two PRA configurations while holding the model,
tokenizer, examples, chunking, and extracted hidden states fixed. The generic
condition scores each mean chunk gist by normalized cosine similarity to the
last query state. The learned condition uses a rank-128 asymmetric linear
router trained with a multi-positive softmax objective. Both conditions retain
the rest of PRA's typed-resource, chunk-index, selection, and consumer-layer
machinery. Thus the comparison isolates the routing adapter rather than
comparing PRA with and without memory.

For each exact checkpoint identity, independent 128-token encoding blocks are
divided into 32-token routing chunks. We extract the normalized attention input
from the final decoder layer while retaining each architecture's native
normalization, masks, RoPE, grouped-query attention, and, for Gemma, mixed
sliding/global topology. Identity-disjoint splits contain 48 training, 16
validation, and 32 held-out test examples, evenly divided between QASPER and
HotpotQA. The HF runs use bfloat16 after a finite-feature gate; the gate rejects
non-finite extraction before optimization or publication. Five seeds (11, 23,
37, 53, and 71) are trained for 512 steps; the
seed with the highest combined validation AUC$_{0--30}$ is selected before the
test split is evaluated.

\subsection{Inherited typed-record product policy}

The runtime also forward-ports Paper 7's finalized typed-record policy without
reinterpreting its metrics as layer-profile evidence. Per-record compression
accepts target-token, maximum-token, and ratio bounds. Typed postings, indexed
BM25, and a small embedding index are built independently of the full-body
native Q/K size gate. Above that gate, cheap hybrid discovery returns an exact
selector; only the selected region is eligible for lazy native encoding and
detail-K/V materialization.

In Paper 7's controlled protocol, type-aware compression reduced mean visible
tokens from 180.8 to 54.1, a compact ratio of 25\%. Automatic recovery was
71.9\%, and cheap-hybrid recall at four was 87.5\% over 24 cases. These are
inherited measurements with their original cohort and provenance, not new
Paper 4.5 quality results. Their role here is to define canonical runtime
behavior: full native indexing is budgeted, while cheaper address modes and
exact backing identities remain available above the gate.

The task smoke invokes the inherited Paper 8 task graph, provenance attachment,
and task-structural selector rather than copying expected IDs into the
report. It selects the expected record in all 18 model/condition rows (three
tasks, three models, visible and selected-full conditions). Selected-full
one-token accuracy equals visible-prefix accuracy for every model. Tool and
skill disclosure, safe execution, compact results, and session persistence are
shared control-plane contracts and pass the executed SDK tests and notebook;
we do not label them model-generated tool-use results. The base Llama mirror has
no chat template, so no Llama tool-call claim is made.

\subsection{Caching and reload amplification}

The runtime includes a thread-safe, byte-bounded LRU for opaque warm or hot
payloads.  Besides hits, misses, and evictions, it records loaded and reused
bytes.  We define reload amplification as
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{reload}} = \frac{B_{\mathrm{loaded}}}{\max(B_{\mathrm{resident}},1)}.
\end{equation}
An apparently high hit rate can still be costly if the misses repeatedly load
large references.  Conversely, byte reuse can be high even when object-level
hit rate is modest.  Both views are needed.
Native reusable keys include tenant, user, session, resource, layer, payload
variant, source revision, positional frame, materialization layout, and scope
signature. Raw backing can be reused across compatible encodings, but per-layer,
rebound, and packed payloads require exact geometry identity. The audit accepts
only the identical key and rejects changed source, position, packing, or request
scope. Per-tenant byte caps evict within that tenant before the global
cap is considered, and scope-local cleanup cannot evict another tenant's
entries. A concurrent same-URI fixture confirms tenant isolation under pressure
and cancellation cleanup. This is logical/runtime isolation, not hard GPU
partitioning.

\section{Engine-Portability Model}

\subsection{Agent, gateway, and engine are independent}

The distributed contract has three authorities. The agent owns user-visible
task and session semantics, typed records, provenance, and authorization
references. The gateway owns transport sessions, logical resource mapping,
protocol translation, and explicit capability negotiation. The inference
engine owns tensor layout, physical K/V blocks, device placement, attention,
and scheduling. The normal wire carries JSON logical identities rather than
raw K/V tensors.

The agent boundary is now explicit in code. \texttt{AgentTurnContext} contains
the conventional chat trajectory and separate context, tool, skill, and task
records. \texttt{PRAAgent} performs semantic selection but does not choose a
wire representation. \texttt{NegotiatedRemoteBackend} probes
\path{/v1/pra/capabilities} once at launch or reconnect and resolves TEXT,
PRA-FULL, or PRA-DELTA from advertised features. A reachable endpoint without
the capability route is ordinary OpenAI; an unreachable endpoint is an error,
not a reason to silently downgrade.

The canonical record projection preserves ID, type, URI, version, source
fingerprint, provenance, authorization scope, task ownership/status, available
views, selected view, and sharing scope. It never exports Python policy objects,
native tensors, or physical page IDs. G10 advertises that its immediate gateway
accepts typed records even when its downstream engine is E0; consequently AUTO
sends logical records to the gateway and the gateway owns deterministic text
injection. G11 forwards record, task, and session semantics. An ordinary server
receives no \texttt{pra} field.

The implemented \texttt{PRAWireRequest} preserves messages, tenant/session/task
IDs, resources, facets, budgets, execution-policy hints, provenance, and
required capabilities in a version-1 extension of the OpenAI request.
\texttt{PRAEngineAdapter} reports static capabilities,
prepares and closes sessions, and offers generation plus streaming. Four deterministic gateway modes cover both sides of the migration:
G00 passes ordinary traffic to an ordinary engine; G10 converts explicitly
selected resources to labeled text for a PRA-unaware engine; G01 infers typed
resources from system and tool-result boundaries before calling a PRA-aware
engine; and G11 preserves an explicit PRA request for a PRA-aware engine. G10
is control-plane PRA with text materialization, not native PRA execution.

Capability negotiation is fail-closed. A request that requires native K/V is
rejected by an E0 engine unless it explicitly allows text fallback and selects
G10. The trace then names the unsupported capabilities, downgrade, selected
logical IDs, gateway mode, correlation ID, and whether native K/V was used.
Cross-tenant resources and credential-like request metadata are rejected before
translation. Relevance still does not grant source or tool authorization.
G01 promotes only explicit record boundaries---system records, tool results,
attachments, and already typed messages. It does not infer semantic records
from arbitrary unstructured user traffic.

We freeze four integration levels. E0 receives ordinary selected text. E1
understands logical PRA identity, versions, intervals, authorization, session
deltas, and fallback semantics, but still realizes context through an ordinary
model path. E2 consumes selected resources directly as detached native K/V or
an equivalent non-prefix representation with matched geometry, one attention
normalization, request-local lifetime, and isolation. E3 adds scheduler-owned
placement, prefetch, promotion, eviction, affinity, batching, and distributed
movement on top of E2. The local HF adapter is the E2 semantic reference. The OpenAI-compatible HTTP adapter and standalone
\texttt{pra gateway serve} process implement E0. FreeToken remains reachable
through that E0 surface~\cite{freetokenDocs2026}. Companion Papers 6.1 and 6.2
exercise native SGLang-MLX and MLX-LM E2 mechanisms; their provider declarations
are available here, but their engine measurements do not promote Paper 4.5 to
E3. Paper 6 now exercises native vLLM-Metal V1 generation and
scheduler-disjoint pages under a pinned provider, while its generic provider
remains E0 unless that bridge is explicitly installed. The portable
HF adapter implements E2 native execution and streaming but remains stateless
between gateway calls. The configurable remote E1 envelope carries incremental
messages and resource operations when the selected engine advertises those
capabilities. The HTTP gateway emits OpenAI-compatible server-sent events.
Cancellation is cooperative at token
boundaries; request-owned references are released only after the decode worker
stops. No backing content is included in trace metadata.
The capability object names native K/V, logical references, streaming,
selected-interval materialization, request lifetime, host/device residency,
tenant isolation, phase selection, and scheduler hints independently. The HF
reference advertises phase selection through the constrained cache-correct
handoff above, but still rejects scheduler hints. This prevents an E2 adapter
from being mistaken for an E3 scheduler merely because both accept the same
logical wire request.

\subsection{Session-aware realization and cumulative context reuse}

PRA does not replace ordinary prefix caching. A session may simultaneously
retain a conventional sequential-prefix K/V cache and separately address PRA
records or native detail. The gateway preserves append-only prefixes for
ordinary engines and can send logical deltas to PRA-aware remote engines.
Between selection and request construction, an engine-independent realization
planner reconciles a visible-materialization ledger against the actual
serialized model context. Identity is strict: resource ID, version, selected
interval, rendering profile, and rendered-content digest must all agree.
Compatible occurrences remain in place; dropped, superseded, removed, or
task-closed occurrences materialize again. This rule survives process restart
through a durable logical snapshot, while opaque engine handles and inferred
physical residency are intentionally discarded.

The planner classifies each selected item as already visible, natively
available, requiring materialization, optionally refreshable, or invalid. Its
accounting keeps three quantities disjoint. Logical visibility prevents a
duplicate input occurrence. An engine prefix observation reports only physical
reuse of a sequential prefix and may be confirmed, likely, unknown, or absent.
Native Memory reports selected model-native state only on a qualified path.
Correctness never depends on a likely or unknown prefix hit, and worker,
model-fingerprint, or engine-restart changes invalidate unproven continuity.
The first PRA-DELTA turn sends a full inventory. Later unchanged resources emit
body-free UNCHANGED operations; changed identity, content fingerprint,
authorization, task ownership, or shareability emits ADD or UPDATE, while
retirement emits REMOVE. The gateway retains bodies outside its public trace so
G10 can render unchanged resources without requiring retransmission. Endpoint
restart, reconnect, protocol mismatch, or explicit refresh clears the agent's
acknowledged inventory and forces a full resynchronization before delta mode
resumes.
Figure~\ref{fig:gateway-two-cache} makes these state classes explicit. The
inference engine physically owns the ordinary prefix cache. PRA logically owns
resource identities, versions, addresses, and selection; the physical owner of
their indexes or selected native K/V depends on E2--E3 integration. A hit or
invalidation on one axis therefore says nothing by itself about the other.

The wire contract promotes \texttt{history\_mode}, engine-session identity,
prefix handle, cache-affinity key, maximum decode length, and resource
operations to typed fields. \texttt{FULL} means that the request supplies
canonical complete history; \texttt{DELTA} means only appended messages;
\texttt{AUTO} resolves from capabilities rather than message count. Stable
resources similarly produce \texttt{ADD}, \texttt{UPDATE}, \texttt{REMOVE},
or \texttt{UNCHANGED} operations while preserving ID, URI, version digest,
record type, and authorization scope.

A versioned engine registry resolves OpenAI-generic, vLLM, SGLang, FreeToken,
Ollama, Hugging Face, llama.cpp, MLX, and custom profiles. Unknown compatible
servers safely remain E0, full-history, and cache-telemetry-unknown. vLLM and
SGLang profiles represent automatic prefix-cache capability but do not infer
native PRA from an engine label. Companion E2 mechanism results are registered
through explicit providers and remain separate from Paper 4.5 evidence. The
local HF reference remains E2-native but
stateless across calls; the session/resource-delta row is exercised by a
deterministic remote-engine contract fixture. Explicit configuration may
override registry defaults; resolved capabilities, rather than labels, control
transport.

For G10, the former message-zero synthetic system block remains only as a
legacy experimental control. The default inserts selected evidence into the
current user turn, after the byte-identical prior serialized conversation.
Role-sequence tests cover Qwen, Llama, Gemma, and generic OpenAI-style
transports. This placement preserves template validity and the previous
prefix. It does not imply that changing evidence is cacheable: only the prior
sequential prefix is reusable.

The sidecar is keyed by tenant, logical session, and model. It retains only
ephemeral engine handles, prefix fingerprints, resource versions, policy
digests, and affinity hints; durable canonical history continues to use
\texttt{SessionService}. Preparation is idempotent, explicit close releases
engine state, and cancellation of one stream does not close its reusable
session. Model revision, chat-template, visible-prefix, history-rewrite,
engine-restart, and invalid-handle changes invalidate the relevant handle.
Updating one detached resource emits a resource delta without invalidating an
otherwise stable conversation prefix. The cache-affinity key is only a future
scheduler hint; no worker-placement result is claimed.

\subsection{Agent-side compatibility before native engine work}

The pre-SGLang deployment gate asks whether PRA's logical record semantics can
survive an existing agent without patching its model runtime. The
\texttt{DeepSeekHarnessPRAAdapter} consumes durable tool-result, session-reference,
and attachment events. The \texttt{PiCodingAgentPRAAdapter} consumes Pi's public
tool-completion and completed-message events. Both deduplicate event identities,
retain typed provenance and task/session IDs, and emit the same tensor-free
\texttt{PRAWireRequest} used by the HTTP gateway.

Across \AgentPluginCases{} deterministic five-seed contract cases, the DeepSeek
and Pi bridges each pass \DeepSeekPluginPass\% and \PiPluginPass\% of identity,
typing, task-metadata, deduplication, and explicit-fallback checks. In every G10
case the ordinary engine receives labeled evidence text, zero logical resources,
and no native-K/V claim. These tests validate the integration boundary, not the
quality or latency of either upstream agent.

\subsection{Compilation ladder}

The eager path is the correctness baseline.  The first compiled primitive is a
paired \texttt{index\_select} over K and V with dynamic selected-token count.
Cold compile time and warm latency are separate rows, and parity is checked
against eager outputs.  Failure never falls back into an eager row.

Triton, custom CUDA, and fusion follow profiling rather than precede it.  The
candidate hot paths are interval gather, deduplication/remap, packing, and
position preparation.  A kernel is justified only if it improves the complete
request after launch, transfer, and synchronization costs.  This ordering
prevents a fast isolated kernel from becoming a misleading end-to-end result.

\subsection{Thin vLLM handoff}

PagedAttention showed how virtualized K/V blocks improve serving utilization
and batching~\cite{kwon2023vllm}.  Deep PRA integration would additionally make
logical PRA blocks, semantic residency, and retrieval-aware prefetch visible to
the scheduler.  That is outside this paper's boundary.

The implemented \texttt{VLLMThinBackend} instead emits a
\texttt{VLLMThinRequest}: prompt, stable selected URIs, materialized token count,
and ordinary K/V metadata.  Semantic scores do not cross the boundary.  An
ordinary engine can consume already selected memory without owning retrieval
policy.  When no executor is installed, the backend produces the handoff for
inspection and raises on generation rather than pretending to serve.

\section{Relation to Existing Systems}

PRA is complementary to several kinds of memory optimization.  H2O and SnapKV
select or retain influential cache entries inside an active sequence
\cite{zhang2023h2o,li2024snapkv}.  PRA begins from stable external identities and
query-time addressability, so its inactive detail K/V may live outside the
accelerator.  These methods can eventually share page layouts or eviction
mechanisms, but their selection semantics differ.

Prompt Cache and CacheBlend reuse precomputed prompt or retrieved-knowledge
state~\cite{gim2023promptcache,yao2024cacheblend}.  Their lesson is directly
relevant: recomputation and transfer can dominate.  PRA adds bounded semantic
selection before native K/V consumption.  RetrievalAttention similarly joins
vector retrieval with long-context attention, reinforcing the need to count
index and transfer work rather than only selected attention work.

GQA reduces the number of physical K/V heads~\cite{ainslie2023gqa}.  Our runtime
preserves this layout through warm storage and materialization.  It does not
expand K/V to query-head count as an implementation convenience.

\section{Canonical Evaluation Methodology}

\subsection{Questions and claim levels}

The initial gate asks four narrow questions.  First, does the portable selected-
K/V implementation preserve exact tensor parity?  Second, how large are eager
gather and interval-pack costs?  Third, how much does CPU residency add for the
same selected payload?  Fourth, can the local toolchain execute the compiled
path?  Quality and routing selection remain frozen.

Claims are labeled by evidence.  \emph{Measured} means executed on the recorded
host.  \emph{Inherited} means read from a checked-in earlier experiment with
its own protocol.  \emph{Contract only} means code exists but no external
engine ran.  \emph{Architectural} means only an interface comparison is made.

\subsection{Gateway session and prefix protocol}

The controlled gateway fixture executes five turns with changing selected
records under four conditions: legacy message-zero G10 insertion,
prefix-preserving G10, E0 message deltas, and an E1 logical
message/resource-delta contract. It records whether each prior serialized
message sequence remains an exact prefix, the reusable-message fraction,
invalidations, JSON message and resource bytes, delta bytes, and session reuse.
The workload is deterministic and uses a simulated adapter so that transport
semantics can be isolated from model quality and network variance. Byte counts
refer to the fixture's JSON envelopes, not tokenizer output or K/V allocation.
Physical cache hits remain unknown unless adapter telemetry supplies them.

\subsection{Negotiated agent-transport protocol}

A second deterministic fixture follows one task through open, tool-result,
waiting, resume, and complete turns. Its detached stream contains a long
document, an atomic tool schema, a skill record, versioned task state, and a
large tool result. The conversational spine grows normally. We freeze the same
selected record identities and compare complete TEXT rendering, PRA-FULL, and
PRA-DELTA JSON requests. Reported bytes include OpenAI fields and PRA metadata;
visible-token estimates count message text only. Selection parity, task-status
projection, and provider-native tool-schema preservation are hard assertions.
The fixture does not call a language model, so it measures protocol semantics
and retransmission rather than answer quality or TTFT.

\subsection{Portable primitive protocol}

The new benchmark uses PyTorch 2.12.1 with CUDA 12.6 on Windows 10, Python
3.10.11, and an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 950M with compute capability 5.0.  Each
synthetic tensor has four physical K/V heads, width 64, and 8,192 candidate
tokens in float32.  Selection admits 512 tokens (6.25\%).  Batch sizes are one
and four.  CPU rows use five warmups and 30 timed repetitions; CUDA rows use ten
warmups and 50 repetitions.  Every CUDA timing synchronizes before and after
the operation.

Indexed gather uses evenly distributed selected token IDs.  Interval packing
uses eight intervals under the same 512-token budget.  The hierarchy comparison
holds intervals and output bytes fixed while moving sources among HBM, pinned
CPU, and ordinary CPU.  Compiler errors are artifact rows.  The benchmark is a
mechanism profile, not TTFT or throughput for a language model.

The layout sweep holds 8,192 total source tokens across four references and two
layers.  It packs 32-token pages in layer-major, reference-major, chunk-major,
or block-major order, then restores an identical 512-token logical selection.
Store construction is a cold cost; reported layout gather latency follows the
same warmup and repetition protocol as the other device rows.

\subsection{Inherited profiles}

Two earlier artifacts answer adjacent questions.  The Paper 2 public-API demo
ran frozen Qwen3-0.6B over two QASPER and two HotpotQA examples.  It records
reference ingest, query encoding, routing, generation, K/V fractions, transfers,
and peak GPU allocation.  The Paper 4 lifecycle fixture ran eight deterministic
resources through authenticated cold, warm, and hot states.  Its answer proxy
is required-document availability, not language quality.  We do not pool these
measurements with the new microbenchmark or compute cross-host speedups.

\subsection{Execution-policy mechanism profile}

We instantiate the same random-weight, three-layer tiny Llama configuration for
five seeds and ingest two stable synthetic references. Every condition uses
three active PRA layers, deterministic decoding, and at most three generated
tokens. We compare request/shared at first and last routing layers,
request/per-layer, token/shared at first and last routing layers, and
token/per-layer. The profile records routing and selection epochs, temporal and
layer Jaccard, selected-identity agreement, and generation wall time. It tests
execution semantics and relative routing work, not language quality or
production latency. EOS can terminate before three tokens, so token-level
operation counts are averaged over the actual forwards.

\subsection{RAG engine-mode protocol}

Retrieval and selector quality belong to Papers~3.2 and~3.3.  Here MultiHop-RAG
is only a controlled engine workload: a fixed 50-question, seed-11 cohort and a
tamper-evident receipt hold selected document intervals and token budgets fixed
between realizations~\cite{tang2024multihoprag}.  The engine comparison is
Selected Context (visible text re-prefilled per request) versus Native Memory
(the same selected text encoded as detached MLX K/V), each measured cold and
warm.  We report TTFT, total latency, throughput, ingestion time, visible and
native tokens, retained K/V bytes, peak memory, and output parity.  Selector and
answer-quality artifacts remain available for audit but are not repeated here.

\subsection{Compilation is a negative capability gate}

The \texttt{torch.compile} API is present, but neither local compiled path is
valid.  CPU Inductor cannot find the Microsoft C/C++ compiler.  CUDA compilation
rejects compute capability 5.0 because the Triton backend requires capability
7.0 or newer.  No warm compiled latency is reported and no eager result is
relabeled as compiled.  Triton and custom CUDA therefore remain untested.

\subsection{Semantic storage lifecycle}

A 120-turn mixed trace compares plain LRU, size-aware LRU, and weighted
task/type-aware retention at the same 80 KB WARM quota. Weighted retention
raises the utility-weighted hit rate from 0.700 to 0.796, preserves every
long-running task and shared-document access, and reduces SOURCE
reconstructions from 214 to 184. Its estimated context-resolution cost falls
from 1,170 to 936 ms. These are policy-trace results; the extra Python scoring
work is not presented as engine serving speed.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.76\linewidth]{storage_policy_trace/storage_policy_trace.pdf}
\caption{Equal-quota retention on a mixed task, document, and tool-result
trace. Weighted policy protects active task state and shared documents while

The runtime now makes the cache hierarchy a shared semantic contract rather
than an engine-specific accident. Each typed record has an authoritative
SOURCE representation and may have derived native detail in COLD, WARM, or HOT
form. HOT means attention-ready and request-pinnable; WARM means lossless native
detail suitable for fast promotion; COLD means capacity-oriented persistence
with lossless compression and optional, separately named K/V quantization.
The physical HOT object remains an engine concern---vLLM pages, an independent
SGLang object, or MLX arrays---but record type, task status, dependencies,
reconstructability, grace periods, and quotas are decided once by
\texttt{PRAStorageManager}.

The deterministic policy retains plain LRU, size-aware LRU, reuse-count, and
reload-cost controls, and adds weighted LRU. Its score combines recency,
frequency, reconstruction cost, typed-record prior, task state, authorized
sharing, dependency count, byte cost, and reconstructability. Open, blocked,
or waiting tasks enforce minimum WARM lifetimes. Completion schedules delayed
compaction rather than deletion; an open dependent task cancels aggressive
compaction. Session close releases transient native detail without treating a
native-cache quota as permission to delete SOURCE. Newly encoded detail first
observes a grace period, avoiding speculative persistence of short-lived tool
and terminal records.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The results separate three claims that are often collapsed. Selection can
improve evidence availability and reduce visible context before any engine
change. Native Memory can preserve the same selected evidence while moving it
outside prompt accounting. Native Serving becomes economically useful only
when the engine owns attachment, placement, reuse, and cleanup cheaply enough
to offset its control-path cost. Exactness and token reduction close broadly;
the economic gate remains engine- and workload-dependent.

\section{Cross-Model, Precision, and Product Qualification}

\subsection{Common Qualification Contract}

The qualification contract asks four separate attribution questions.  First,
No PRA versus Selected Context measures PRA routing and visible-context
construction.  Second, Selected Context versus Native Memory holds selected
record IDs and intervals fixed and isolates native-K/V realization.  Third,
Native Memory versus Native Serving holds selection and representation fixed
and measures scheduler-owned placement, reuse, and lifecycle.  Fourth,
no-adaptor versus bundle comparisons hold execution mode fixed and isolate the
exact model-specific bundle.  We use stable condition identifiers for all
seven mode/adaptor combinations.  In particular, E0 is Selected Context and is
already a PRA condition.

The available flagship engine cohorts primarily answer the second question.
True matched No-PRA arms for Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, and
Llama-3.2-1B remain \texttt{NEEDS\_RUN}; exact bundle-versus-no-adaptor
end-task arms are also absent.  We therefore do not attribute the large E0/E2
visible-token difference to PRA versus ordinary inference.  It is only the
effect of realizing the same PRA-selected evidence as native memory.

All engine rows now answer one question: at fixed or acceptably changed
quality, when does PRA reduce total serving cost? The benchmark computes each
query, candidate set, selected IDs, and selected intervals once. A cryptographic
selection digest is then reused by \texttt{FULL}, \texttt{E0\_SELECTED},
\texttt{E2\_HOT}, and \texttt{E2\_WARM}; optional E3 conditions add prefetch,
remote WARM, COLD, or SOURCE restoration without rerunning retrieval. Context
size, shared versus independent resources, and concurrency 1, 4, 8, and 16 are
manifest dimensions. This separates retrieval quality from representation and
transport.

Every row pairs task quality with serving measurements. Binary workloads derive
successful requests per second as request rate times task success and report
successful tasks per accelerator-hour. Monetary cost remains null unless an
auditable hourly-cost source is supplied. Visible-token reduction and active-K/V
reduction remain separate. Tail percentiles, physical memory components,
transfer bytes and time, cache events, reuse, hardware metadata, status, and
provenance are first-class fields. The current legacy registry has no row with
both request rate and task success, so its quality-adjusted-throughput field is
honestly \texttt{NOT\_MEASURED} rather than reconstructed after the fact.

Figure~\ref{fig:engine-maturity} distinguishes a validated level from a deeper
observed mechanism. In particular, AirLLM has a live E2 mechanism smoke but
remains E0-qualified until natural quality and larger-than-VRAM evidence close
the gate; SGLang has local E3-like HiCache controls but lacks distributed,
scheduler-owned E3 validation. Raw throughput is deliberately absent from this
maturity axis.

\subsection{Agent-level configuration and interactive surfaces}

Low-level \texttt{pra.yaml} controls model/context execution. A separate,
versioned agent profile controls model endpoint, workspace, durable sessions,
tool approval, skill directories, MCP declarations, task policy, and generation
budgets. Resolution is explicit CLI options, selected named profile, default
profile, project config, user config, then package defaults. The short option
\texttt{-p} selects an agent profile; \texttt{-P} independently overrides PRA.
Credential files remain server-side references and are redacted from session,
inspection, benchmark, and model-card output.

Agent profiles now independently select
\path{context.transport=auto|pra|text}, text-fallback permission, and required
features. The CLI exposes \path{--context-transport} and
\path{--allow-text-fallback/--no-text-fallback}; \path{-P/--pra} continues to
select the model/runtime PRA profile and is not overloaded with wire semantics.
The launch summary, TUI \path{/runtime} command, and web session diagnostics
report requested and resolved transport, capability source, gateway mode,
integration level, resource-delta support, and native-K/V status without
including record bodies.

\texttt{pra agent chat} is the stable TUI reference and can resolve a default
profile without model flags. \texttt{pra agent run} provides a noninteractive
form. The optional FastAPI/WebSocket UI is another presentation layer over the
same \texttt{PRAAgent}, \texttt{AgentLauncher}, session, task, and authorization
services. It supports multiple durable conversations and sequence-based replay
after reconnect. A dangerous tool call remains blocked until the backend
receives a typed one-shot approval; the browser cannot bypass host policy. The
UI binds to loopback by default and is experimental rather than a scientific
contribution or production security claim.

The fleet Control Plane follows the same separation at deployment scope. REST,
streamable-HTTP or stdio MCP, the built-in Control Plane agent, and an embedded
operator CLI call one transport-neutral \texttt{ControlManager}. Its fleet,
Registry, deployment, action, qualification, observability, context, experiment,
and audit managers own authorization and policy; engine and Registry clients
remain behind backend protocols. A declarative operation catalog maps semantic
operation IDs to permissions, side effects, and risk, and drives REST route
exposure and MCP tool discovery. A disabled operation is therefore absent from
OpenAPI or MCP discovery rather than merely failing after dispatch. The default
MCP surface is disabled; when enabled it is read-only except for plan
construction, with apply and experiment submission separately denied.

Operational mutation uses a common plan--apply transaction. The plan records
current and projected state, impact, reversibility, restart requirement,
required permission, confirmation requirement, tenant, expiry, and idempotency
key. Plans and completed results are durable, so REST, MCP, and CLI retries share
the same idempotency decision. Manager-owned audit records add roles,
permission, request and trace IDs, transport, before/after state, and outcome.
An official MCP SDK protocol test initializes both stdio and authenticated
streamable HTTP, discovers the configured tools, and invokes the same fleet
manager used by REST. This is interface validation rather than a serving-quality
or latency claim.

\subsection{PRA under agentic coding workloads}

The coding-agent campaign uses end-task success as the admission gate and
context economy only as a secondary objective. Terminal-Bench 2.1 is delegated
to Harbor 0.22.0 with frozen task identifiers, fresh task containers, official
verifiers, and preserved agent-native context policy. An oracle first passed
the downloaded \texttt{filter-js-from-html} task, establishing that task setup
and grading were functional. OpenCode 1.18.26 with Ollama/Qwen3-14B then
completed all five No-PRA smoke trials without harness exceptions, but solved
0/5 tasks while passing 7/12 individual verifier tests and consuming 100,883
cumulative input tokens. We therefore did not promote that pairing into a PRA
profile sweep.

The follow-up campaign makes that sequencing executable rather than relying on
operator convention. Each published baseline pins source and model revisions,
harness, cohort, scaffold, engine, precision, context and step limits, decoding
policy, and official grader. A resumable scheduler writes a durable receipt
after every state transition. Gateway pass-through, Selected Context, and
native-PRA cells cannot launch until their exact No-PRA dependency is marked
\texttt{BASELINE\_REPRODUCED}. A smoke subset or a changed engine,
quantization, or context window remains
\texttt{BASELINE\_ATTEMPTED}; it can qualify infrastructure but cannot authorize
a PRA efficacy claim.

Gateway placement is not synonymous with PRA execution. We therefore factor
the promoted agent experiment into two binary ownership axes. The ordinary
controls are direct engine access and G00 pass-through to that same ordinary
engine. The native comparison is (i) an agent sending typed selected context
directly to a PRA-capable engine and (ii) the same agent using a G11 PRA
gateway in front of that engine. This defines two experiments that must not be
conflated. In \emph{G11-equivalence}, the complete request and selected-resource
envelope are replayed, and matching resource digests make the difference an
estimate of gateway mediation. The direct run writes an ordered selection
fixture keyed by a digest of the complete agent-visible request. Replay has no
ordinal or approximate fallback: a changed trajectory aborts the equivalence
cell instead of admitting a different selection. In \emph{G11-end-to-end}, each placement owns
its normal session and selection path; this estimates product behavior, with
selection and transport jointly present. Both fix the engine, model revision,
task and repeat, budget, profile, and official grader. Easy-50 contains both
the fail-closed frozen replay and a separate route-owned product cell. G10
remains a separate selected-text fallback and is not relabeled as native
execution.

Every Easy-50 arm fixes a 4,096-token per-turn completion ceiling in addition
to the 32,768-token context and 50-step limits. This ceiling is part of the
execution fingerprint. It bounds pathological continuations when a backend
does not emit its normal end marker; ordinary shorter actions are unchanged,
and a ceiling hit is retained as a protocol event rather than a PRA quality
outcome.

The executable version-2 matrix records connection, engine-PRA and gateway-PRA
flags, gateway mode, physical engine target, and comparison group in every row.
It refuses native labels unless endpoint preflight reports effective native
K/V, and it completes each direct task before launching its gateway mate.
Paired reports count outcome agreement, regressions, recoveries, token traffic,
and wall time. Mini-SWE-agent and Qwen Code are the first enabled external

403,703 prompt tokens, and 9,503 completion tokens, versus 63 calls, 458,690

Native profile qualification then exposed three agent-history correctness bugs.
First, mini-swe-agent can reject a malformed assistant action and append a
format-error user observation without retaining the rejected action; the live
KV sequence must therefore support exact-prefix backtracking rather than assume
append-only history. Second, independently selecting message fragments can
retain two assistant actions while omitting the intervening tool observation,
which Qwen's chat template rejects. Third, treating only the latest observation
as mandatory can still discard the assistant command that produced it. On the
task-01 trajectory this changed the second generated response and immediately
caused a format error. The corrected selector ranks historical atomic causal
turn bundles: one assistant action plus all following user/tool observations up
to the next action, while the complete active action--observation tail is
mandatory. The task statement remains pinned, selected bundles retain causal
presentation order, and the complete reconstructed request must pass the
physical model's chat template before resource materialization. Intermediate
frozen replay
of the admitted task-01 trajectory validated every one of its 31 user-ending
request prefixes at each of the 75\%, 50\%, and 25\% profiles (93 rendered
requests total). This validates template grammar, not causal completeness or
task efficacy; both fragment-level runs and profile attempts made before the
active-tail correction are excluded from solve-rate and efficiency deltas and
must be rerun.
The revised segmenter also treats message/tool records as the first physical
boundary. Short records remain intact; overlap never crosses records; and an
oversized observation splits at file headers, stack frames, test cases, or diff
hunks before falling back to token windows. Every child retains a stable parent
record and causal-group ID plus return-code, mutation, and result metadata.
Child slices are disjoint before budget accounting, while physical engine
packing preserves their logical identities. Once engine correctness qualifies,
the matched segmentation ablation will compare fixed token windows,
record-aligned messages, causal bundles, and hierarchical causal bundles with
selective regions inside oversized observations.
The engine path also stopped deleting the pinned selected-history sequence when
its digest changes. It submitted the replacement text to the same owned slot
and let llama.cpp retain the exact common token prefix while trimming and
re-evaluating the divergent tail. A physical M4 probe cached 2,450 of 2,455
replacement-prefix tokens and evaluated five tokens versus 2,455 under cold
materialization. This is useful composite-prefix reuse, but it does
\emph{not} qualify agent-history PRA: any selected text after the common prefix
is encoded again, and several selected records are collapsed into one physical
resource. We therefore reclassify this path as a rematerialization diagnostic.

The first corrected active-tail efficacy result separates those layers. On
task 01, both the same-engine plain control and the 100\% live-prefix PRA arm
resolve the task in 31 model calls with exact trajectories. The 75\% selector
matches them through two generated actions, then diverges when it drops the
oldest completed turn on request 3; it submits after 23 model calls but its
nonempty one-line patch fails to apply, for 0/1 officially resolved. Estimated
logical-to-physical context falls by 23.2\%. Independently, resource-delta
telemetry records 83,644 selected-prefix token-exposures but only 33,600 newly
evaluated resource tokens, with 43,852 exact common-prefix cache hits, three
identity hits, 18 delta updates, and no physical K/V copy. These counters
describe ordinary longest-common-prefix reuse of changing selected text, not
arbitrary selected-record K/V reuse. This selector profile also fails its
task-quality gate. The row is retained only as a diagnostic and does not admit
an end-to-end gateway or PRA efficiency claim. The task-01
50\% arm also resolves 0/1: it reaches the 50-step limit with no patch while
reducing estimated logical context by 47.0\%. Its 167,908 resource-managed
selected-prefix token-exposures require 57,301 newly evaluated tokens; 83,775
tokens hit exact delta prefixes, 13 requests are identity hits, 34 are delta
updates, and no K/V copy occurs. The 25\% arm likewise resolves 0/1 at the
50-step limit with no patch. Its pinned active tail limits realized estimated
savings to 67.6\%, but delta/identity reuse reduces newly evaluated resource
tokens to 19,193 across 116,572 resource token-exposures. A frozen-selection
cache on/off factorial was planned, but the Easy-50 matrix is now stopped until
the live-prefix subset implementation passes the correctness gate below.

An initial frozen factorial appeared to fail a stricter consumption gate. The
cache-off 50\% anchor submitted an unresolved nonempty patch after 44 model
calls and 507.9~s. The cache-on arm replayed the same selected records and
logical requests for three calls, but its third response differed and request
4 consequently had no matching frozen digest. A subsequent 100\% audit found
that this was not yet a pure prefix-cache factorial: cache-on continued the
live full-prefix slot, whereas cache-off routed the lossless selected history
through detached resource materialization. We therefore retain the run only as
an implementation diagnostic and exclude its partial score and runtime ratio.

The repaired adapter treats 100\% retention as a semantic no-op. If no live
slot is consumed, it validates and reconstructs the complete ordinary chat
prompt and cold-prefills that prompt directly; it does not enter detached
resource materialization. With llama.cpp revision \texttt{458681e1}, batch 256,
microbatch 1, and Qwen3-Coder-30B Q4\_K\_M, a frozen 30-turn trajectory then
matched cache-on and cache-off on all 30 continuations while all 29 eligible
cache-on requests reported physical hits. More importantly, the autonomous
task-01 control now matches on all 24 logical-request hashes, ordered-selection
digests, and complete responses. Both arms emit the same 689-byte, 14-line
patch and resolve under the official grader. Cache-on retains 120,848 of
126,772 physical prompt tokens (95.3\%) and completes the agent in 100.3~s,
versus 2,792.5~s cache-off, a 27.85$\times$ single-task speedup. This is a
qualified strict \emph{prefix-cache versus cold-prefill} control, not a PRA
speedup: 100\% retention selects no subset and the cache-on arm remains on its
ordinary live slot.

\paragraph{Agent-history K/V correctness reset.}
We stopped the Easy-50 treatment matrix after recognizing that the composite
resource design violated the product invariant. For an agent trajectory, PRA
must select cells already encoded in the session's live prefix cache; it must
not tokenize and prefill the selected transcript again. The replacement
llama.cpp path names one pinned live source sequence plus multiple stable
record ranges. Unified-cache \texttt{seq\_cp} adds request membership to those
existing cells without copying K/V, preserves their original positions, and
removes request membership without deleting source membership. Newly evaluated
query and generation cells are committed back to the source. The final sampled
token, which llama.cpp returns before evaluating it into K/V, is explicitly
reported as pending and bridged in the next suffix.

On an M4 Pro with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct Q4\_K\_M, a 20-turn same-state gate
forked the control and treatment from identical source K/V on every turn. All
20 continuations matched token-for-token as the source grew from 11 to 343
tokens; zero selected tokens were re-encoded and every suffix commit succeeded.
The record-aware agent wrapper then exercised a genuinely sparse turn: from a
531-token live source it attached 26 K/V tokens (4.90\%), evaluated none of the
selected history text, copied no K/V, and returned the pinned key correctly.
A separate rejected-action path removed the discarded branch by rolling the
source from 27 to its 23-token common prefix, then committed the recovery suffix
with zero selected-text re-encoding and returned the correct recovery answer.

The first current-build real-agent gate uses predeclared baseline-success task
02, \texttt{django\_\_django-\allowbreak15368}; task 01 was not substituted after both its
matched current plain and selected-K/V executions became 0/1 under the revised
binary.  The task-02 ordinary-prefix-cache control and the 100\% live-K/V arm
both resolve 1/1 in 14 model calls, emit the same 673-byte, 13-line patch, and
match on all 14 assistant responses byte-for-byte.  After the initializing
request, the live arm continues the canonical same-slot sequence on every turn:
54,686 resident K/V token-exposures are reused, only 2,996 wire-suffix tokens
are evaluated, selected-history text is never re-encoded, and no physical K/V
copy occurs.  Wall time is 141.4~s for the plain control and 184.1~s for the
instrumented live arm, so this row establishes correctness, not speedup.  An
earlier replay of task 01 also matched both controls through 19 responses, then
failed closed because an \texttt{ls -la} observation carried different file
timestamps and therefore a different exact request hash.  That is a frozen-
observation harness defect, not a model-response divergence; replay fixtures
must freeze tool observations or key them by stable causal identity.

At 90\% budget the same task passes through four exact full-retention actions.
The first sparse request attaches 3,047 of 3,221 resident source tokens and
drops the oldest completed causal turn while retaining the task, active tail,
and two most recent turns.  Response 5 changes in wording but issues the same
command; the subsequent trajectory edits the intended line, then submits an
invalid 503-byte patch after 12 calls and resolves 0/1.  The run realizes only
10.2\% estimated logical-context saving.  This is the first measured sparse
trajectory failure on the corrected K/V mechanism, but it is not yet
attributed solely to selection.  In the frozen consumption control, live K/V
and fresh prefill match exactly on the four full-retention requests.  At the
first sparse request, compact fresh prefill consumes 3,543 tokens while the
live-K/V request spans 3,717 logical positions: the 174 omitted tokens leave
positional holes because the native path deliberately preserves original
record coordinates.  The responses differ slightly in reasoning text but
issue the same command.  Thus ``identical selected records'' is insufficient
to isolate K/V construction unless positional presentation is fixed too.

A position-preserving fresh-evaluation control now closes that narrower
correctness question.  It cold-prefills the frozen 3,221-token source, attaches
the identical 3,047 selected K/V entries to a clean request sequence at their
original positions, and evaluates the same 496-token wire suffix.  Its response
is byte-for-byte identical to the live-K/V response at the first sparse request
(matching SHA-256
\texttt{f9b4fceb64011a3c79a0c607f8e0b5efec23e3a32c89ea0d9db6d66b8cd45cdf}).
Consequently, the native path is reusing the intended K/V subset without
selected-text re-encoding or K/V-construction drift.  The earlier compact-fresh
difference is a positional-consumption effect.  The task-level 0/1 result can
therefore be assigned to the sparse treatment as a whole, but selection loss
and original-position versus compact-position consumption remain separate
factors for the next frozen factorial.

The paired stochastic control strengthens this single-seed result.  After an
exact replay of the four source-building turns, five predeclared sampling seeds
(101, 202, 303, 404, and 505; temperature 0.7; 128-token continuation cap) fork
from the same 3,047 selected cells.  Each live-built continuation is
byte-for-byte identical to its position-preserving cold-built counterpart;
the five seeds produce five distinct response hashes, and all five pairs match.
This clears the multi-seed live-versus-fresh K/V-construction gate for this
model, request, and physical slot geometry.  It does not establish quality at
90\% retention, because both arms consume the same sparse state.

Cross-model replication exposes a necessary qualification dimension.  With
Qwen3-14B Q4 and its default model template, turn 2 fails closed before
inference because the template inserts an empty thinking wrapper when an
assistant record is final, then removes that wrapper after a later user record
arrives.  The completed assistant record is therefore not token-prefix-stable,
and its old K/V cannot represent the next transcript.  With llama.cpp's stable
ChatML template, the same probe builds a four-turn causal source twice with
identical response hashes, attaches 3,069 of 3,205 source cells, and obtains
byte-identical live-built versus cold-built continuations for sampling seeds
101, 202, and 303.  Model identity alone is consequently insufficient:
agent-history qualification binds the model, tokenizer, chat template, and
engine geometry.  The direct endpoint now runs a four-record prefix-stability
probe at startup and rejects an incompatible template before the first costly
agent prefill.

That control exposed a separate occupancy confound.  With both request slots
empty, two frozen full-transcript prompts produce identical response hashes
under cache-on and cache-off execution.  Replaying the exact physical selected-
record payloads is also exact.  If the native readiness probe first leaves an
unrelated seven-token resource pinned in sequence 0, however, the cache-off
response to prompt 2 changes reproducibly even though sequence 1 is erased,
reports zero cache hits, and receives the same 1,857 prompt tokens.  The changed
hash exactly reproduces the earlier apparent cache mismatch.  This is unified-
KV physical-occupancy sensitivity, not evidence that the selected transcript
or ordinary prefix cache is wrong.  Readiness probes are now ephemeral and
release their resource sequence before measurement.

The first concurrent sparse-session attempt then exposed a related ownership
bug rather than clearing the gate.  The wrapper assigned canonical histories to
distinct source slots, but built them as ordinary prefix-cache requests without
setting llama.cpp's persistent PRA pin.  Under unified-KV scheduling,
\texttt{try\_clear\_idle\_slots()} consequently purged idle source slot 1 while
the other session was active: the slot-3 request attached correctly, whereas
the slot-1 request failed closed with ``source has no encoded context.''  The
fix marks only canonical live-history sources with
\texttt{pra\_pin\_resource}; ordinary no-PRA controls remain unpinned.

After that repair, two Qwen3-14B/ChatML sessions independently built three-turn,
1,262-token live histories in source slots 1 and 3.  Their simultaneous
75\%-budget requests used destination slots 0 and 2, each attached five records and
662 existing K/V cells, re-encoded zero selected-history tokens, and copied no
K/V.  Both complete source trajectories and both sparse target responses were
byte-identical to a separately rebuilt sequential control.  Concurrent target
wall time was 182.9~s versus 296.0~s sequential, but this 1.62$\times$ observed
scheduling ratio is diagnostic only: an unrelated virtual machine and endpoint
security substantially contended for the host during the run.  The result
clears cross-session slot ownership and behavioral-isolation correctness for
this qualified configuration; it is not a sparse-runtime speed claim.

The small-model rows and the task-02 result are mechanism and single-task
correctness gates, not Easy-50 population-level efficacy evidence.  A strict
frozen replay now resubmits the successful task-02 arm's recorded physical
payloads and observations: all 14 assistant responses, including mutation,
verification, and submission turns, reproduce byte-for-byte.  This removes
live filesystem timestamps and other tool nondeterminism from the replay
verdict and clears the single-task frozen-agent gate.  Together with the
qualified Qwen3-14B replication, this clears the current single-task,
multi-seed, cross-model construction, and concurrent-session isolation gates.
The wrapper additionally serializes same-session requests, makes exact
transport retries idempotent, rejects a different generation from the same
logical head as a commit conflict, applies resource deltas transactionally,
and tombstones a session before draining and erasing it.  A direct Qwen3-14B
probe confirms those semantics: an exact retry returns the cached identical
response; two simultaneous requests from one logical parent produce exactly one
HTTP 200 commit and one HTTP 409 conflict; DELETE completes in 4.4~ms after the
winning request, and later traffic receives HTTP 410.  This clears retry,
same-session commit, and completed-request termination.  It does not clear
active cancellation by itself.  To close that gap while preserving the public
non-streaming API, the wrapper consumes llama.cpp's completion stream
internally, retains the live upstream response handle, and closes it when the
session DELETE arrives.  A repeated direct probe observes the upstream stream,
returns HTTP 499 \texttt{generation\_cancelled} to the interrupted request,
returns HTTP 200 to DELETE in 1.54~s, and records llama.cpp cancelling and
releasing the active task.

The eviction/offload gate uses a one-pair Qwen2.5-0.5B configuration so slot
reuse is unavoidable.  The wrapper saves a 24-token canonical sequence as a
295,916-byte llama.cpp slot image, erases both sequence memberships, and the
next pressure session occupies the released source slot.  Restoring the first
session into that same pair, with its PRA pin set atomically by the restore
operation, yields a byte-identical continuation to the never-offloaded control.
No selected text is submitted for prefill, and the checkpoint file is deleted
after successful restore.  This clears explicit disk demotion, capacity reuse,
restore identity, and cleanup for the tested runtime.

That final mechanism gate uses ten alternating-order pairs on the same small
model and fixed synthetic trajectory.  Ordinary full-prefix continuation reads
all 1,495 resident cells; sparse live-K/V attaches 391 cells (26.2\%) across
five causal records, with zero selected-text re-encoding and no K/V copy.  The
one-token target response is identical in every pair.  Median engine prompt
time falls from 174.0 to 161.4~ms (1.08$\times$ ratio of medians; 1.09$\times$
median paired ratio), while endpoint wall time falls from 294.7 to 266.7~ms
(1.10$\times$; 1.11$\times$ paired); sparse wins all ten prompt and wall pairs.
This qualifies an engine-specific sparse runtime benefit at this geometry, not
an Easy-50 task-quality or large-model speedup.  The llama.cpp live-agent path
therefore clears the construction, concurrency, lifecycle, offload, and sparse-
runtime gates.  A campaign-level lock prevents the resumable Easy-50 scheduler
from launching treatment cells before that status is explicitly promoted.  The gate is now
\texttt{QUALIFIED} only for a llama.cpp wrapper with prefix caching enabled and
a configured slot checkpoint directory; cache-off wrappers and HF, MLX, vLLM,
and SGLang remain unqualified.  The restarted efficacy sequence operates on the
14 baseline-success instances, one instance at a time: each instance must first
pass the 100\% semantic-no-op control before its 90\% treatment can run, and
lower retention remains contingent on that result.

The restarted task-01 row illustrates why that protocol separates mechanism
parity from current task capability.  Its 100\% live-K/V execution makes 30
model calls, re-encodes zero selected-history tokens, copies no K/V, and reuses
234,035 resident K/V token-exposures.  A frozen ordinary-prefix replay of the
same recorded requests and tool observations reproduces all 30 assistant
responses byte-for-byte.  The independently autonomous plain run instead
matches the first 22 responses and first differs after an \texttt{ls -la}
observation carries a changed directory timestamp; with frozen observations,
that apparent divergence disappears.  Both autonomous arms nevertheless score
0/1 because this current model/binary pairing submits explanatory prose rather
than an applicable unified diff.  We classify that as a matched capability
failure, not a PRA regression, and do not spend a lower-retention efficacy row
on it.  The sequence advances to the next instance that reproduces its admitted
baseline solve; historical membership in the 14-task stratum and current paired
success are reported separately.

This also changes the comparison with ordinary prefix caching. Prefix caching
can reuse only the common leading run and must either keep every middle turn or
evaluate a replacement suffix when selection changes. Live-record PRA keeps
the canonical full session once, adds request membership to selected existing
cells, and in the sparse smoke exposes only 26 of 531 source cells to the new
request. Thus a selection change no longer implies thousands of re-prefill
tokens. It may still change the number of K/V cells read by attention and the
amount of resident memory, so four quantities remain disjoint: logical token
retention, selected K/V attachment, newly evaluated tokens, and wall time. The
first three now have physical telemetry; no speedup is claimed until a matched
agent run shows that reduced K/V reads dominate membership and residency costs.

The same frozen agent history also shows why transcript rematerialization
cannot be assumed equivalent across engines. Table~\ref{tab:agent-cache-smoke}
reports diagnostic token parity; continuation scopes differ and the rows are
neither speed nor quality rankings. The bounded HF arm is exact despite small
logit changes, while MLX, vLLM, and SGLang each expose prompt-dependent token
changes. MLX precision is non-monotonic, so quantization alone does not explain
the effect. Product adapters must preserve the live K/V state that generated
the trajectory, or explicitly qualify a strict rematerialization mode; they
must not silently substitute one for the other.

\section{Negative Results and Next Gates}

The current evidence is intentionally incomplete. The local HF compilation
probe uses an old CUDA GPU, while companion experiments span two Apple hosts,
one newer CUDA laptop GPU, and an Intel GPU. Bounded async promotion,
multi-session pressure, prefetch lead, and request-tail distributions are now
measured; production continuous batching, a stable upstream native-page ABI,
live LMCache, fused segmented attention, online cancellation under load, and
sustained multi-tenant serving are not. The LRU fixture validates byte
accounting but is not a production cache policy study. The inherited language-model cohort is
too small for quality claims.  The new capability and compact-result workflows
and Paper 8 task/session integration are executed contract tests, not a new
latency or answer-quality experiment. The primary interactive shell remains
single-agent. The SDK now includes Paper 9's experimental lineage, selective
visibility, generic effect, resource-validity, and native-reuse contracts so
external harnesses can wrap their own child loops; Paper 9 retains ownership of
the subagent benchmark and qualification claims. Learned long-term memory
remains scoped to Paper 10.
Model-resident result routing currently requires an isolated model session; safe
concurrent multi-tenant partitioning of model-resident result K/V remains an
engine-level gate.
The multi-axis HF bridge serializes high-level generation on one wrapped model
to isolate temporary adapter state. It is a correctness reference, not a
continuous-batching implementation. The cache key now rejects physical reuse
across changed source revisions, positions, materialization layouts, or scopes,
and a concurrent fixture covers logical tenant isolation, pressure, and
cancellation. It does not establish hard GPU partitioning or production
multi-tenant throughput. Phase selection and portable E2/E3 engine integration remain
open. The G01 parser recognizes explicit
system, tool, and attachment boundaries; it does not claim semantic recovery
from arbitrary opaque traffic.

Papers~3.2 and~3.3 own the document-RAG retrieval, routing, transfer, and quality
evidence. Paper~4.5 imports their product boundary without reproducing those
experiments: immutable contiguous-block reuse is qualified, while independent
record composition and reduced materialization remain
\texttt{CALIBRATION\_PENDING}.

The Paper 4.5 gateway and agent-transport profiles are deterministic transport
experiments. They do not by themselves measure physical prefix-cache hits,
continuous batching, or engine queueing. Companion experiments now measure
local physical reuse and controlled two-host SGLang affinity, but the latter is
not wired through online generation. The reported gateway reuse
fraction counts exact serialized messages, not tokenizer tokens. Engine
profiles are conservative defaults and require runtime probing or explicit
configuration before stronger capabilities may be advertised. Companion
engine studies do not change this profile's evidence tier and are intentionally
kept outside Paper 4.5's HF-centered metric tables.

The cross-model cohort contains only three one-token continuations per model.
It is sufficient to expose exact disabled behavior, K/V topology, position,
lifetime, and coarse semantic drift, but not to estimate broad language quality.
The Llama row uses a public weight mirror because the pinned official repository
was inaccessible. Gemma is partial rather than passed: its global-only adapter
cannot make external K/V visible to unchanged local sliding layers. Sparse
profiles are declared per family and are not assumed to preserve quality.
The layer calibration reuses that diagnostic cohort. Its 102 rows vary
profiles, not independent examples, so they identify gross transport drift and
set conservative SDK defaults but cannot estimate downstream answer quality.
Qwen last-24's target-token gain is especially vulnerable to this small-cohort
selection effect. It is consequently exposed only as
\QualityMaxCandidate{} with pending calibration, not as
\QualityMax. Paper 3 owns retrospective placement evidence on MuSiQue and
2Wiki; a future calibration should add held-out QASPER, HotpotQA, typed-result,
and long-generation workloads before any sparse profile becomes a default.

The next experiment should move to a compute-capability 8.0 or newer GPU with a
pinned software stack.  It should first reproduce eager parity, then measure
\texttt{torch.compile} cold and warm paths.  Only measured hot spots should move
to Triton.  Layout and hierarchy sweeps should cross contiguous versus indexed
selection, whole chunks versus intervals, GQA head count, batch heterogeneity,
and warm-cache locality.  End-to-end runs should report TTFT, inter-token
latency, throughput, peak HBM, off-device bytes, temporary bytes, transfers,
and reload amplification together.

The next policy experiment should expand the trained-model retrieval smoke,
hold selected-token budgets fixed, and compare answer quality, TTFT, inter-token
latency, selection churn, and routing-layer disagreement over a powered cohort.
The implemented phase/shared handoff closes the cache-correctness prerequisite:
the routing probe sets \texttt{use\_cache=false}, the seeded selection is
consumed by one cache-producing prefill, and selected detail is never inserted
into the ordinary local cache.  A request guard binds selected keys to tenant
and session identity and rejects cross-scope or duplicate attachment.  Its
remaining gate is workload-scale calibration and production multi-tenant
throughput rather than mechanism availability.

Deeper engine integration remains outside Paper 4.5. Companion engine papers
have established SGLang and MLX native mechanism baselines and controlled vLLM
generation on Metal and CUDA. A shared matched benchmark now closes E0/E2
semantic parity for all three engines. The corrected vLLM-Metal geometry reaches
3,360/3,360 exact cross-family pairs, while expanded Qwen confirmations preserve
2,086/2,086 per engine. SGLang and MLX still expose unfused decode overhead on
reused paths. Those results remain environment-specific and do not turn the
generic provider into an unconditional E2 claim. Native APC and offline
concurrency now pass on pinned Metal and CUDA paths when cache identity includes
native selection and geometry; online queued concurrency, LMCache, broader
positions, masks, GQA, and heterogeneous batching must pass before portable
speed comparisons. Controlled off-node WARM transfer and affinity now pass
outside the SGLang generation path; engine-owned distributed placement remains
open.
If useful behavior requires semantic residency, retrieval-aware scheduling, or
cross-request PRA block sharing, that work belongs to the deep engine paper.

\section{Conclusion}

PRA's selected-memory fraction is an algorithmic fact, not a systems result.
This paper turns that distinction into code and measurement.  One runtime now
connects the public model API, authenticated memory lifecycle, typed resources,
lazy tool and skill disclosure, safe execution, compact result backing,
selective replay, durable tasks and sessions, physical K/V planning, caching,
profiling, and backend handoff. Typed results build full native indexes only
within configurable token/byte ingestion budgets; larger records retain cheap
recovery interfaces and promote selected regions lazily. Its policy lattice
makes routing frequency and physical lifetime
inspectable, while the gateway permits ordinary and PRA-aware agents and
engines to coexist without sending native tensors over the logical wire.
The agent no longer flattens selected records before remote execution: AUTO
preserves typed context through G10, G11, and direct PRA endpoints, while an
ordinary endpoint receives one deterministic text rendering. In the five-turn
task/tool fixture, PRA-DELTA cuts wire bytes by
\AgentTransportDeltaTextReduction{} relative to TEXT and sends
\AgentTransportDeltaBodies{} rather than \AgentTransportFullBodies{} resource
bodies, without changing selected identity or task metadata.
At 50 turns, including reconnect and resynchronization, the reduction grows to
\AgentScalingDeltaTextReduction{} versus TEXT and
\AgentScalingDeltaFullReduction{} versus PRA-FULL. A ten-example MLX-LM
QASPER cohort then preserves exact model input, output, and gold-answer
log-probability across TEXT, PRA-FULL, and PRA-DELTA in \AgentModelParity{}
cases. The protocol result is therefore behavioral as well as structural.
Conventional sequential-prefix K/V and detached PRA resource/native state are
now separate capability and lifecycle axes. The tested gateway preserves every
eligible prior prefix in its five-turn fixture and sends typed message/resource
deltas through a prepared session, while correctly leaving physical cache hits
unknown. The
HF semantic reference now passes full-prefix logit equivalence on the tested
Qwen and Llama checkpoints, plus record-boundary deduplication and request-long
decode-lifetime gates. Gemma validates native MQA mechanics but identifies a
global/local topology boundary rather than a full semantic pass. Layer
placement is now a versioned calibration result rather than an implicit ``PRA
layer'' convention. Separate address, detail-K/V, routing, and consumer sets
make persistent storage and request-time computation independently auditable;
missing detail can never trigger a silent profile downgrade. The eager
primitives are exact and inexpensive in isolation, but
off-device transfer multiplies their cost on the measured host.  Compilation
remains a negative gate. Companion engine runs establish pinned native
realizations but still leave portable E2/E3 integration unmeasured. Under the
RAG workload, frozen contiguous MLX selections are output-exact: retained K/V
cuts total latency by 78.9\% for Qwen3-4B at 2K, 86.9\% at 4K, and 82.1\% for
Qwen3-8B, while cold native execution is 1.8--3.3\% slower. Independent chunks
remain an experimental composition mode because their outputs are not exact.
The practical
conclusion is conservative: cache locality, physical residency, and context
lifecycle deserve attention before kernel novelty, and every future speed
claim must match semantics while accounting for the whole request.

\section{Reimplementation Guide}

The stable public entry points live in \texttt{src/pra\_hf/runtime.py}.  A
reimplementation needs five small contracts.  First, represent warm K/V as
equal-shaped tensors $[B,H_{kv},T,D]$.  Second, express selections as stable URI,
layer, and half-open token intervals.  Third, merge overlaps before applying a
hard global token budget.  Fourth, pack selected tensors by consumption layer
without expanding grouped-query heads.  Fifth, emit stage times and physical
bytes independently of routing-quality metrics.

The model adapter only needs \texttt{add\_reference}, \texttt{generate}, and
\texttt{inspect}.  The thin serving request contains selected identities and
materialized-token metadata, never semantic scores.  Tool execution receives a
separate authorization object after discovery.  These boundaries allow another
runtime to reproduce the interface without adopting the Python implementation.

Paper 8's finalized geometry adds one further invariant. A frozen selection is
an immutable set of source-local anchors. Expansion may change the interval
width or choose the complete selected record, but it cannot cross record
boundaries or change source identity. Overlap is merged before budgeting, and
each consumer resolves the same logical interval to that layer's own projected
K/V. The query starts after the longest source extent, while separate records
retain source-relative positions. Raw intervals are retained for diagnostics;
the canonical union is the only geometry materialized. The named
\texttt{paper3\_default}, \texttt{paper7\_selected\_detail}, and
\texttt{paper8\_full\_record\_diagnostic} profiles resolve to ordinary runtime
configuration. The public sequence is \texttt{route}, \texttt{freeze\_native\_selection},
\texttt{plan\_native\_materialization}, then
\texttt{generate\_with\_native\_plan}. Task operations follow the analogous
control boundary: parse untrusted proposals, validate them against a copy of
the task graph, and only then replay accepted events through the session service.

\subsection{Agent transport implementation map}

The semantic/wire split is implemented in
\path{src/pra_hf/agent_transport.py}. \texttt{AgentTurnContext} keeps messages,
context records, tool records, skill records, provider-native tool schemas, and
task metadata independent of transport. \texttt{context\_record\_to\_wire\_resource}
is the only logical projection; \texttt{render\_text\_messages} is the only text
compatibility renderer. \texttt{CapabilityNegotiator} distinguishes a reachable
ordinary server from an unavailable endpoint and caches the version-1 feature
handshake. \texttt{NegotiatedRemoteBackend} resolves the mode, computes message
and resource deltas, sends the OpenAI-compatible envelope, and clears its
acknowledged inventory on refresh.

The call site is \path{src/pra_hf/agent.py}. \texttt{\_turn\_context} separates
conversation records from detached records and projects active task state;
\texttt{\_generate\_turn} delegates either to the negotiated backend or the
canonical embedded text renderer. Thus selection and tool authorization remain
agent responsibilities, while rendering and network serialization remain
transport responsibilities.

The public wire dataclasses and protocol-version check are in
\path{src/pra_hf/deployment.py}. Gateway capability composition and G10/G11
mediation are in \path{src/pra_hf/gateway.py}; acknowledged message/resource
inventory, body-free unchanged operations, and resynchronization state are in
\path{src/pra_hf/gateway_session.py}. The compatibility matrix is executable in
\path{tests/test_agent_transport.py}; the five-turn byte fixture is
\path{experiments/paper4_5_runtime/run_agent_transport_protocol.py}.

\subsection{HF native-reference implementation map}

The four-role resolver is in
\path{src/pra_hf/layer_profiles.py:1--430}. Lines 27--77 define independent
encoding and missing-detail policies; lines 81--178 normalize all, suffix,
fractional, evenly spaced, and explicit profiles; lines 181--244 validate the
$L_A,L_{KV},L_R,L_C$ invariants and serialize provenance. The stable public
fields and registry precedence are applied in
\path{src/pra_hf/config.py:19--420}. The versioned model entries live in
\path{src/pra_hf/model_profiles/layer_profile_registry.json}.

Lines 328--510 of the HF \texttt{injection.py} module separate reference
payloads. Every injected layer can produce
compact identity metadata for cross-layer mapping, address layers retain their
search representation, and only detail layers retain native K/V. The cache
record exposes \texttt{has\_detail\_kv} in
\path{src/pra_torch/memory.py:99--140}; materialization rejects an address-only
chunk before attention in \path{src/pra_torch/core.py:232--270}. Selecting a
chunk ID at $L_R$ never licenses reuse of $L_R$'s tensor at $L_C$: the identity
is resolved to the destination layer's independently projected K/V.

The complete geometry is concentrated in
\path{src/pra_hf/native_geometry.py:59--374}. Lines 59--107 define four
materialization modes and the three named research profiles. Lines 130--169
make raw and unique interval accounting, source lengths, and query offset part
of the immutable plan. Lines 189--238 clamp expansion to one record; lines
310--374 normalize overlap before slicing each consumer layer's own K/V.

The high-level path is in \path{src/pra_hf/model.py:932--1191}.
\texttt{plan\_native\_materialization} separates routing anchors from selected
chunk, asymmetric window, selected-record, or full-scope geometry.
\texttt{native\_next\_token\_logits} is the E0 diagnostic.
\texttt{generate\_with\_native\_plan} is the public fixed-plan path, and
\texttt{debug\_decode\_with\_native\_plan} spells out prefill, local cache
handoff, and one-token decode calls. All three use
\path{src/pra_torch/hf/injection.py:494--529} to start the direct query after
the longest active source extent.

At attention time, \path{src/pra_torch/hf/qwen.py:340--352} prepends selected
native K/V to local K/V and records compact request-lifetime state before the
family-native eager kernel. Llama and Gemma 3 inherit this shared path while
overriding only family symbols and native scope. The trace owner is defined in
\path{src/pra_torch/hf/adapter_base.py:107--129} and aggregated at
\path{src/pra_torch/hf/injection.py:228--241}; no full K/V or attention tensor
is retained for telemetry.

Portable streaming begins in
\path{src/pra_hf/deployment.py:227--341}. The worker owns logical references,
the cancellation criterion, and cleanup. Gateway mediation and SSE framing are
separate at \path{src/pra_hf/gateway.py:131--231}, so a transport disconnect
closes the iterator without exposing backing content. Tenant-scoped cache
identity and local eviction are in \path{src/pra_hf/runtime.py:568--672}.
Cache identity includes source revision, positional and materialization
signatures, and scope; equality is the reuse predicate rather than a heuristic
URI match. Engine capability fields separately expose interval materialization,
request lifetime, residency, tenant isolation, phase selection, and scheduler
hints. An independent implementation should fail a required unsupported field
before reference registration.

\subsection{Gateway session implementation map}

The independent capability axes begin at
\path{src/pra_hf/deployment.py:38}: the capability record carries engine type,
PRA level, prefix-cache mode, session state, incremental-message support,
resource deltas, affinity, and explicit handles. Typed request serialization
starts at line 129, and the profile-parameterized OpenAI adapter at line 242.
The versioned defaults and validation are in
\path{src/pra_hf/engine_profiles.py:1--90}; adding an engine requires an enum
member and one conservative registry row before any feature override.

Ephemeral state is isolated in
\path{src/pra_hf/gateway_session.py:186--434}. \texttt{resolve\_turn} compares
explicit full or delta history, derives resource operations, and computes the
affinity key. \texttt{commit} advances fingerprints only after a successful
decode. It stores resource IDs, digests, and metadata, never content tensors.
The gateway execution order is visible at
\path{src/pra_hf/gateway.py:147--457}: resolve and invalidate, prepare once,
transform G00--G11, execute, commit trace state, and close explicitly. HTTP
capability, session-debug, and session-delete handling follows at lines
460--538. The same registry entry points are surfaced by
\path{src/pra_hf/runtime.py:1194--1220}, preventing the HTTP process from
becoming the sole owner of session semantics.

\subsection{Execution and deployment code map}

Line references identify the artifact version accompanying this paper.
\path{src/pra_torch/execution.py:61--107} defines the four policy axes and
their validation; lines 358--425 define tensor-free identities and row-local
plans; lines 428--504 own request epochs, churn, and trace summaries; and lines
507--632 separate selection reuse from opaque materialization lifetimes.
The important invariant is the type boundary: a selection controller returns
URI/chunk spans, while only an engine resolver may return tensors or block
handles.

The canonical typed-result admission policy is implemented at three layers.
\path{src/pra_hf/adaptive_context_runtime.py:82--236} defines global and
per-record token/byte limits, inheritance, deferral, and serialization.
\path{src/pra_hf/progressive_context.py:58--66} defines the four lifecycle
states; lines 347--391 define the public audit and lazy-region records; and
lines 602--773 perform idempotent size-gated full indexing and selected-region
native encoding. The facade in
\path{src/pra_hf/runtime.py:1119--1268} applies automatic preparation at
ingestion and exposes preparation, lazy promotion, and lifecycle inspection.
This order is important: policy is resolved before model-native construction,
and a gate rejection preserves the backing identity and recovery interfaces.

\path{src/pra_hf/hf_execution.py:49--174} is the family-independent HF
bridge. At the routing layer it turns the current hidden representation into a
logical plan. For shared scope it remaps the source-layer hits through
\texttt{map\_chunk\_identities\_to\_layers}; each destination therefore receives
its own native grouped-query K/V. The public request lock and precedence entry
point are in \path{src/pra_hf/model.py:710--895}. Qwen, Llama, and Gemma call
the same bridge from their thin attention adapter.

For a distributed implementation, begin with
\path{src/pra_hf/deployment.py:100--174}: reproduce that JSON request without
adding tensor fields. At lines 175--182, expose capabilities, session setup,
generation, streaming, and close as separate methods. Then reproduce deterministic mediation in
\path{src/pra_hf/gateway.py:24--178}: negotiate first, transform according to
G00--G11 second, and append component-local trace events last. The HTTP process
starts at line 180; the CLI binding is
\path{src/pra_hf/gateway_cli.py:35--59}. This order keeps an unsupported
native request from being mistaken for successful text fallback.
The agent bridge implementation is
\path{src/pra_hf/agent_plugins.py:1--253}; its family-specific parsers terminate
in the common logical request builder rather than engine calls. Frozen interval
expansion and layer-native slicing are in
\path{src/pra_hf/native_geometry.py:1--374}; the SDK facade delegates through
\path{src/pra_hf/runtime.py} so users do not access adapter internals.
